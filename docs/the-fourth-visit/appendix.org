* How the Story Was Built

** The seed

A housing manager with a growing repair backlog and no authority over the
people who do the repairs has six weeks to find out why every improvement
has made tenants wait longer.

** The five sentence story

Corinne Vance is handed the repair backlog at Harbor Row Housing, where
every team is hitting its targets and tenants wait thirty-four days. She
gives the board a convincing explanation, that tenants are not home, and the
notices sent on the strength of it reach an old man her own staff failed
four times, who is found cold and taken to hospital. She sets out to prove
the real cause is a stock cut, is taken apart in front of the director, and
learns that the contractor hired on her first explanation has cost
forty-eight thousand dollars while the tenants go to the city. She finds the
conflict that keeps the real cause in place and a way to dissolve it, but
the trial breaks the one number the city watches and the director stops it.
With the people who must change now holding the reasoning, she offers the
hearing a sealed expectation instead of a defense, is partly wrong, and
finds that the difficulty has moved.

** The characters under pressure

Corinne's talent is being believed. Twice it lets her carry a wrong answer
into a room. Her test is whether she can hand her reasoning to someone who
wants to break it, and at the end whether she can say "I expect" where she
used to say "because".

Odile plans berths at the port and wants her father warm. She will not ask
for a favor, because she knows what a favor does to a queue. She asks
questions, is away when Corinne most wants telling, and never gives an
answer about a business she does not know.

Bram ended a humiliation by proving his technicians work all day. Lu ended
a year when tenants got an answering machine. Wendell freed two hundred
thousand dollars that was sitting in bins. Gloria needs something true
enough to act on by Thursday. Each of them is right, and each one's success
is part of the cause.

Mags can do five visits or two repairs. Samira wants a fast answer and a
finished job and refuses to be told she must choose. Between them they find
the change.

** The scene map

Part One, /Busy/, gives Corinne a list, an aim and four conditions, and
ends with her taking the largest trouble to the board as its own cause.
Part Two, /Two Explanations/, puts a man in hospital, teaches her to join
troubles with "if" and "then", lets her misuse that to convict a colleague,
and ends at a wall of yellow notes with one statement under five of her
seven troubles. Part Three, /The Conflict/, finds why that statement has
lasted, lets her split the difference, makes her say her own assumption from
six years before, and then shows what the change costs the call center.
Part Four, /The Test/, is about order, a first action that costs her
something, and an envelope.

* The Thinking Processes in the Story

The chapters avoid the method's vocabulary on purpose. Here it is.

Goldratt posed three questions: /what to change?/, /what to change to?/ and
/how to cause the change?/ The diagrams that answer them are known as the
thinking processes; Dettmer's /Logical Thinking Process/ adds a first one for
the aim.

** The questions Wendell asks

The categories of legitimate reservation are the fair ways to doubt a
causal diagram. In order of appearance: clarity (what does this box mean?),
entity existence (is it a fact?), causality existence (does this really
produce that?), cause insufficiency (is it enough on its own?), additional
cause (what else could produce it?), cause-effect reversal (which came
first?) and predicted effect existence (if this were so, what else would we
see?). An eighth, tautology, guards against using the effect as the only
evidence for its cause; Corinne commits it in chapter 4, when "no access"
is both the trouble and the proof that tenants are out.

** The story's reasoning

- *Aim.* A fault a tenant reports is fixed soon and stays fixed.
- *Conditions.* We know what the repair needs before we go. The person and
  the parts that can finish it arrive together. The tenant can let us in at
  a time they agreed. The money lasts the year.
- *Shared cause.* The job is booked and sent before anyone who could fix it
  knows what it needs. With a van that carries less and a day with no time to
  fetch anything, the visit ends without a repair, and is closed with the one
  code that asks no questions.
- *Conflict.* To repair homes soon, every tenant must know quickly that they
  are being dealt with, so book the next free slot at once. To repair homes
  soon, every visit must finish the repair, so hold the booking until the job
  is understood.
- *Assumptions given up.* That a tenant feels heard only when a van arrives;
  that a repair cannot be understood except in the room; that understanding
  takes days.
- *Change.* The first conversation is with someone who can fix it. As
  altered by Lu: agents take that conversation themselves for the twelve
  commonest faults, from cards written with a technician, and own the promise
  of a call back within one working day.
- *What it cost.* A number the city watched fell from 91 to 66 percent in
  six buildings, and was replaced by one that counted what tenants had rung
  about.
- *What moved.* The wait went from the door to the first-call desk, and the
  next limit is what people know.

** Try a different story

Take a situation of your own in which everyone is working hard and the
result is getting worse. Write the one number each group is proud of. Then
write the number nobody has. Start at step 1.
