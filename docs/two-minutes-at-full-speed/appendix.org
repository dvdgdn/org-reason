* How the Story Was Built

** The seed

A translator who has collected every method for a sharper mind and practised
none has ten weeks to follow two minutes of spoken German she can already
read.

** The five sentence story

Maren Solberg, with a box of unused methods, agrees to interpret at an audit
and asks an expert on practice which method has the best evidence. He makes
her name one scene and try it, and at a conversation table she follows one
word in five, after her firm has told the client she will do the job. While
he is away she fills her hour with a memory game and flashcards whose scores
climb, tells her partner not to book a professional, and three weeks later
follows no more than before. He returns; she learns to tell score from
ability, seals a guess, changes the exercise, and for her second complaint
he shows her a practice with no evidence that he has kept secret for
nineteen years. She sorts everything she owns by the proof it owes, misses
her own guess by one, does the job in the form she can actually do, and is
left knowing how to become able and not yet what for.

** The characters under pressure

Maren does everything a careful person does before beginning, and never
reaches the end of before. Her weakness is a number that goes up. Her test
is whether she will write down a guess she expects to miss, and say what she
cannot do before someone has to find out.

Keane has spent a career asking experts to account for their practice hour
by hour, and has kept one practice of his own in a drawer for nineteen
years. He will not claim more for it than "I think". Showing it costs him.

Ines wants claims that survive. She is right about the memory game, right
that a feeling of progress is the cheapest result there is, and right that
nineteen years was long enough to find out. Jutta will not slow down. Pri
has an hour, ten weeks and a client. Wim opens the laptop because a number
went up today, and nobody in the story tells him he is wrong.

** The scene map

Part One, /The Box/: a count, one scene, twenty scenes, and four minutes at
a real table. Part Two, /The Score/: an exercise, a source of correction, an
assistant, a log, and then five weeks spent on the two things that give a
pleasant number. Part Three, /The Dark/: what each number was a number of, a
sealed guess, a changed exercise, and the bottom drawer. Part Four, /The
Check/: four squares of masking tape, forty points, and two minutes.

* The Method in the Story

** Its usual names

| Steps | The usual name | In the story |
|-------+----------------+--------------|
| 5–6, 12 | Deliberate practice: a well-defined task at the edge of ability, with immediate feedback and repetition | Ten seconds, then thirty, at about half right |
| 6 | Deliberate against purposeful practice | The two piles on Keane's table |
| 8 | A practice log | Attempt, correction, next time |
| 9 | Spaced repetition and retrieval practice | Ines's three curves |
| 10 | Transfer: near and far | "What went up?" |
| 11, 15 | A prediction registered in advance; calibration | The envelope in Ines's drawer |
| 12 | A plateau | Seventy-one |
| 13 | Image cycling | The bottom drawer |

Anders Ericsson, whose work Keane's is modeled on, kept the term
/deliberate practice/ for fields that have established training methods and
teachers who can assign the next exercise, and used /purposeful practice/
for focused work with feedback where no such field exists. Chapter 6 turns
on that distinction.

** The two complaints, as conflicts

Maren makes two complaints. Each is a conflict between two things she
needs, held in place by an assumption.

/"Real practice needs a teacher."/ (chapter 6)

- To become able in fact, I must practise at the edge of what I can do,
  with informed and prompt correction.
- To become able in fact, I must begin now with the means I have.
- For the first, I must wait until I find someone who knows.
- For the second, I must practise alone.
- /Because/ only a human expert can choose the exercise and correct the
  attempt.

What gives way: the material can correct in seconds, an assistant can find
the pattern across attempts, and a real setting once a week says whether
either is telling the truth. And where no field exists there is no teacher
to wait for; the only correction is a guess written down first.

/"I am grasping in the dark."/ (chapter 13)

- To become much more capable, I must aim my practice at a definite target.
- To become much more capable, I must be honest about what I can now
  understand.
- For the first, I must specify the ability before I practise.
- For the second, I must leave it unspecified, and drift.
- /Because/ to aim at an ability one must be able to describe it from where
  one stands.

What gives way: a scene can be pictured without being understood. Maren's
first card describes the room from outside because she cannot see it from
inside, and it serves anyway, twice: as one of the pictures she runs each
morning, and as the thing her check looks for.

If you keep these in a commons, enter each in the /conflict/ tree: the aim
as =objective=, the two needs as =need=, the two actions as =prerequisite=,
and the "because" as an assumption on the relationship it sits under.

** What is known, and what is not

The story sorts methods into four squares. Where each sits in the story:

| | A tool or a count can score it | Only you can report it |
|---+---+---|
| Shown to work for the thing practised | Spaced, self-testing review; practising the skill itself with feedback; memory techniques for the material memorized; chess study for chess; juggling for juggling | — |
| Wider benefit disputed or untested | Working-memory games such as dual n-back; relational-skills training; speed reading | Image cycling; image streaming; "multiple mentality" exercises |

These placements were written from general knowledge of the research. They
have not been checked against each source for this novella, and you should
read before relying on them.

- *Spacing and self-testing* are among the most replicated findings in the
  study of learning. See Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) and Cepeda and
  colleagues (2006).
- *Working-memory training* reliably improves performance on the trained
  task. Whether it improves anything else has been disputed since Jaeggi and
  colleagues (2008) reported that it did; later reviews, among them
  Melby-Lervåg and Hulme (2013) and Simons and colleagues (2016), found
  little or no benefit beyond tasks like the trained one.
- *Image cycling* comes from William Bengston and Bennett Mayrick, who used
  it as part of a method of hands-on healing, described in Bengston's book
  /The Energy Cure/. Bengston has published experiments on healing in mice.
  Nothing published tests whether image cycling affects thinking, learning or
  performance. Bengston himself says the list of wishes was made as a tool
  for cycling and not as a way of bringing wishes about. The story does not
  claim that it helped Maren, and you should not read it as saying so.
  Nothing in this novella is advice about health.

** Try a different story

Take one thing you have wanted to be able to do for years. Count what you
have collected about it and the longest run of days you practised. Then
name a Tuesday.
