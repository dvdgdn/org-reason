#+title: Two Minutes at Full Speed — become able to do it
#+startup: showall
#+options: toc:nil num:nil

/Two Minutes at Full Speed/ is a novella in which Maren Solberg, a
translator with a banana box full of methods for improving her mind and a
record of four days' practice, agrees to interpret at an audit ten weeks
away. She can read every word the engineers will say and cannot follow one
sentence in five when it is spoken. She learns how to choose a skill,
practise it with correction, tell a rising score from a growing ability, and
find out whether the practice changed anything outside itself. The whole
novella is here, one chapter to a page, and each chapter is followed by an
exercise. By chapter 16 you have a practice of your own, a check you can
fail, and a way to sort any method you meet by the proof it owes you.

You need nothing but this and a notebook. If you have read /Three Hundred
Seats/ or /The Fourth Visit/, or keep the practices they teach, each page
says where they meet this one.

* How each page works

- *The chapter* :: the story itself.
- *What Maren has just learned* :: the chapter's lesson in a few lines. It
  gives away nothing from later chapters.
- *Your practice — Step N* :: the exercise in general terms, for a notebook
  and a clock. It is complete in itself.
- *The same step in Org Reason* :: the same exercise with Org Reason's
  commands, on your real records. Paragraphs marked /If you keep a trusted
  system/ and /If you have worked out what limits you/ are for readers of
  the other two novellas. Skip them otherwise.
- *The same step in Reason Commons* :: on steps 11 and 14 only, where the
  step involves claims other people might share or dispute.
- *Carry forward* :: what to keep for the next step.

Do the steps in order, on one skill. Step 4 has to happen out in the world.
Steps 10 and 15 need another person to judge.

The story is in four parts: The Box (chapters 1–4), The Score (5–9), The
Dark (10–13) and The Check (14–16). The [[file:appendix.org][appendix]] shows how the story was
built, sets out its two central complaints as conflicts, and says what is
and is not known about the methods in it. Read it last.

* What you have at the end

- *A target you can stand in* (steps 1–4): one scene in place of a wish to
  be better, a wider list of what you want, and the particular things that
  stop you, found by trying.
- *A practice* (steps 5–9): one short exercise at the edge of what you can
  do, correction that reaches you in seconds, an assistant or a teacher for
  the pattern, a log, and spacing for what must be kept.
- *A way of not fooling yourself* (steps 10–13): a check in real conditions,
  a guess sealed beforehand, the habit of changing the exercise when the
  number stalls, and a way to try something unproven without pretending it
  is proven.
- *A decision* (steps 14–16): every method you own sorted by what it owes
  you, your hours divided, a result, and a weekly review.

* Before you start with Org Reason

You need Org Reason 0.2.0 or later with =org-reason-mode= on (see [[file:../install.md][install.md]]).
The keys assume the default prefix, ~C-c r~; ~C-c r ?~ shows the menu.

Org Reason records practice entries, freezes predictions before their
outcome and keeps your weekly review. It does not schedule flashcards, play
training games or talk to an assistant; you use your own tools for those and
carry the results across. It makes no claim that any practice improves
anything. It keeps what you expected beside what happened.

* A note on evidence

The story takes positions on what is known. They are stated carefully in the
[[file:appendix.org][appendix]], with sources, and they were written from general knowledge of the
research, not from a fresh reading of each paper. One practice in the story,
image cycling, has no evidence for any effect on thinking; the story says so
and does not decide whether it works. Nothing here is medical advice.
