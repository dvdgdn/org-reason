* The chapter

The quote from Sergio came while Maya and Eli were on the floor of Weller with
a roll of butcher paper. They were trying to find out how many minutes it took
to put down a tablecloth, clear a place and put down another. Jo had let them
use one table. Eli had brought two stacks of plates. Maya spread the paper
over the first stack and called it a meal.

Sergio’s attachment was one page. On the bottom right, under /Added cost/, it
said $4,800.

She rang him. He was in the kitchen. Something that sounded like pebbles was
pouring into a pan.

“Is that for the same number of dinners?” she asked.

“Three hundred plates.”

“I thought we were paying by the plate.”

“For food, yes. The schedule is on the second tab.”

“I have a PDF.”

Sergio put the pan down. “You’re asking the same people to serve one party,
clear the room, set it for another party, serve that one, and clear it again.
If you give me till eleven I can use the first crew for more of it. At nine
thirty I need another set of hands.”

Maya held up a plate. Eli held out his hand for it. Maya gave it to him, then
took the phone away from her ear.

“How long was that table?” she asked him.

“Four minutes,” Eli said. “Two people. No glasses, no dirty forks, no spills.”

“How many tables?”

“Fifteen, each time.”

Sergio was still on the line. Maya asked him to send the second tab. He said
he would. Before he hung up, he asked if he should hold the extra crew. She
looked at the total again and said to hold off on booking them. She would give
him a decision when she had talked to Ruth.

On the drive back to the pantry, she thought of the menu. There was the
chicken, which could be made cheaper by taking off the sauce, and the beans,
which wouldn’t save much if they gave people fewer. They could print nothing,
use the back of the original tickets for name tags, fill the water glasses
from jugs. She could find a hundred dollars in the next hour. She tried to
think of where the other forty-seven hundreds were.

Ruth listened from the packing room doorway. Maya showed her the dining room
split, with the two circles for the parties. Then she showed her the line in
the quote for the extra crew.

“Same tickets sold?” Ruth asked.

“Same.”

“Same money from the bank?”

“Yes.”

Ruth pulled the adding machine tape out from under the stapler. “What stays
with us?”

Maya took out her phone. Fifteen thousand five hundred minus forty-eight
hundred. She showed Ruth the result.

“Say it to me,” Ruth said.

“Ten thousand seven hundred.”

They could serve every meal. They could take all the chairs out by nine
thirty. At the end of the evening Ruth would have thirty-three hundred dollars
less than she needed for winter deliveries.

“Suppose we find the thirty-three hundred,” Maya said.

“Where?”

“The bank. They put in five. They’ve been asking if we have a sponsor for the
next one.”

“Ask them, then,” Ruth said. “And if they say no?”

Maya could hear the freezer fan. She remembered that she still hadn’t written
to the building manager about the switch. It was a clear, lovely little job.
She could put it on the list and do it while she waited for Denise. She even
had a photograph of the switch. She drew the notebook out of her bag. Next to
/Sergio — cost and crew for two dinners/, she had the date of his answer and a
line that ended in a check mark.

Ruth took the drawing. “When you know what happens if they say no, bring me
that too.”

* What Maya has just learned

The caterer's answer arrives, and it is $4,800 more than the plan can carry.
In her notebook the waiting-for line ends in a neat check mark, though the
project now has nowhere to go. Maya feels the pull of a "clear, lovely little
job", an email about a freezer switch. Ruth asks what happens if the bank says
no.

* Your trusted system — Step 9: Keep every project moving

/Ten minutes./

An answer you were waiting for has to be acted on. If you tick it and stop,
the project stalls, and usually nobody notices for days.

1. Go down your Waiting For list. For every answer that has come in, cross off
   the line and immediately write what it changes: a new next action, a new
   waiting-for, or a decision that the project is finished or dead.
2. Go down your Projects list. Put your finger on each one and find its live
   step: a next action, a waiting-for, or a date on the calendar. A project
   with none of the three is stalled. Give it a step now.
3. For your most uncertain project, write the fallback: "If the answer is no,
   then I will ___." You should know that before you ask.
4. Look at your Next Actions. Is there a small, pleasant one you keep choosing
   while a harder one sits? Do the harder one's first two minutes today.

* The same step in Org Reason

1. Press ~C-c r a~ and look at the WAIT block. For each answer that has
   arrived, press ~RET~ to open the entry, add a line under the heading saying
   what the answer was, then ~C-c C-t~ and choose DONE.
2. Press ~C-c r d~ for the dashboard. The section "Projects missing an
   available action, wait, or dated commitment" is step 2 above, done for you.
   Press ~g~ to refresh it after changes.
3. Follow each listed project's link (~C-c C-o~) and add its next step as a
   NEXT heading inside the project, as in step 6.
4. Write the fallback as a line under the project heading:
   =If no: ask for two weeks more and cut the second print run.=

The dashboard can tell that a project has no live step. It cannot tell that a
step is vague or the wrong one. That judgment is yours.

* Carry forward

No project without a live step, and one written fallback. Come back to this
check whenever an answer arrives.
