* The chapter

Maya typed /An update about the Winter Supper/ and sat for a while with the
cursor after /Supper/. In the paragraph below it, she’d used the word /change/
twice without saying what had changed. She read the first sentence aloud. If
she were reading it in the line at a grocery store, she would have put the
phone in her pocket before she found the news.

She tried again.

/On Saturday at Weller Hall, we’ll serve light food at two receptions in place
of the three-course dinner you bought. You can come at five or seven, subject
to space, or get your full $75 back. Please tell us by Friday at five. We’ll
call if we haven’t heard from you. We’ll only hold a seat once we’ve confirmed
your time./

Under that she put the address, the ramp and drop-off directions, and the
number Denise would staff. She asked Denise to read it and tell her what a
caller couldn’t answer from it.

“Who needs to ask for the refund?” Denise said.

“Anybody who paid.”

“What if I bought a seat at Maren’s table?”

“Then we need to find out who paid for the table.” Maya opened the ticket
list. Maren had paid for ten together. Two other hosts had used a group name,
but each guest had paid on their own. “We should say where the refund goes.
Back to whoever paid us.”

“What if they don’t pick up?”

“We try again. If we can’t reach them, we send the money back.”

Denise took a copy and marked the line about the door. She asked Maya to leave
the name /Doris/ off the sample they’d read from. The callers would use the
names on the guest list. Maya hadn’t noticed that she’d left Doris’s name in.
She printed one clean copy and took it upstairs where the pantry phone didn’t
ring.

Maren answered on the fourth ring. Maya knew she was on a break because of the
rattle in the background. It was the sound the clinic’s ice machine made when
it filled its tray.

“You have a time for me?” Maren asked.

“I do. There’s something I need to tell you before you choose it.”

She told her the offer. Maren listened without interrupting. When Maya
finished, there was a clink, as if she’d set a glass down.

“I told them it was a proper dinner,” Maren said.

“That’s what we sold you.”

“One of the women is leaving. We’ve been trying to get her out with us for a
year. She had to get a sitter for this.”

“If it doesn’t work, I can send the whole table back. Or you can tell me how
many still want to come and I’ll refund the rest.”

“Was Weller actually booked when you sent the email?”

Maya put her hand on the ticket list. Maren’s name was near the top. She had
bought on the first day.

“Jo was holding the date for us,” Maya said. “She was checking the drawing. I
told the bank to send the address before she told me how many the room could
seat.”

Maya heard the ice machine start another cycle. Maren hadn’t said anything.

“I thought the room would fit,” Maya said. “I should have waited.”

“Would you have told us if you could have made it work?”

Maya looked down at the first sentence she’d tried to write. She had started
with the pleasure of welcoming everyone to Weller.

“I don’t know,” she said. “I wanted to send one email that made it look as if
we’d got there without any trouble.”

Maren’s voice changed a little. “Well. I have to tell them what I know now.”
She asked Maya to send the details in a message she could forward. Four of her
people would probably come for Ruth, she said. The other six she’d have to
ask. Could Maya give her till Friday afternoon?

“I can give you till five,” Maya said. “I’ll call you at three if I haven’t
heard. Will that leave you time to ask anybody you’re missing?”

“Three is good. I’m off at two.”

The next host asked for all ten refunds before Maya got to the times. The one
after that wanted to know if his daughter could still play piano. When Maya
said she could, he said they’d come to both if she needed them. Maya gave his
daughter a time to arrive and wrote down the question about whether Jo had a
stool that went high enough.

At four, Denise brought in a sheet with four names and the times each person
could call. The volunteer at the desk showed her where they could sit. Maya
sent the correction and watched the first replies appear. She didn’t open
them. She called Doris.

“Is it still the same door?” Doris asked when Maya finished.

“Same door. At five, if you want it.”

“I know what to tell Lenny, then,” Doris said. “Five.”

Maya asked her to say hello to him for her. Then she put five in the column
where Eli had left a space.

* What Maya has just learned

Maya's first draft uses the word "change" twice without saying what changed.
Her second says it in the first sentence: light food at two receptions in
place of the dinner you bought, five or seven, or your $75 back, tell us by
Friday at five. On the phone a table host asks whether the hall was really
booked when the first email went out. Maya tells her the truth, and gives her
a time: "I'll call you at three if I haven't heard."

* Your trusted system — Step 13: Change a promise properly

/Ten minutes, and one message sent./

There are three honest things to do with a commitment: keep it, finish it, or
renegotiate it. If you let one lapse without saying anything, you have broken
it.

1. Look through your lists for one promise you can't keep as you made it. There
   is nearly always one.
2. Write the message. In the first sentence, say what you promised and what
   has changed. Then: what you can offer now, what choice the other person
   has, and the time by which you need their answer.
3. Read it as someone standing in a queue would. Could they act on the first
   two lines alone? Ask one other person what the message leaves unanswered.
4. Send it. If the person will be hurt by it, phone them.
5. On your Waiting For list write their name, what you asked, and when you
   will call if you hear nothing. Tell them that time.

If they ask what you knew and when, answer the question.

* The same step in Org Reason

1. Find the promise: ~C-c r a~ and read the NEXT, WAIT and project blocks with
   one question, "can I still do this as I said?"
2. Draft the message in any buffer. Org Reason will not send it. Use your mail
   or your phone.
3. When it has gone, select the sent text and press ~C-c r C~. Choose
   =delegate= and give the person's name. The words you actually sent are now
   stored with the waiting entry.
4. Open that entry from the agenda and press ~C-c C-s~ to set the time you
   said you would call.
5. On the original action or project, record what replaced it: edit the
   heading to the new promise, or ~C-c C-t~ and choose CANCELLED, which asks
   for a note. Write why.

- [ ] One renegotiated promise is recorded as WAIT, with a person and a check
  time that the other person also knows.
- [ ] The old version of the promise no longer appears as live anywhere.

* Carry forward

One promise honestly changed, and a habit worth keeping: whenever you ask for
an answer, give a time and say what you will do when it comes.
