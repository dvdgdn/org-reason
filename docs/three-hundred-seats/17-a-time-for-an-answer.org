* The chapter

On Monday Ruth and Maya sat at the borrowed desk with the final invoices. The
freezer shut off. Maya could hear somebody in the next room counting cans.
They got to twelve and started over.

The hall and the food had cost what they’d said they would. The bank’s payment
was in. Denise had sent the settlement for the tickets with a line for each of
the thirty-one refunds. Ruth ran down the columns with the point of a pencil.
Maya had two numbers in front of her, one in the file they’d been working from
and the other on a tape still warm from the adding machine. They both said
$14,375.

“What’ll you do with the three seventy-five?” Maya asked.

“Put it against fuel. If there’s something left in March I’ll see about the
latch.”

She took down the route schedule, took a copy and put the original back on the
wall. Then she took the copy to the phone. The first driver picked up while
Maya was still gathering the receipts.

“Are you still good for Wednesdays?” Ruth asked. She listened. “Yes. All the
way through March.”

Maya took the invoices into the hall. She’d printed a label for the envelope,
with the event and the year, so she could find them next time. She’d also
written /call Maren/ in the notebook. She looked at it for a while. Maren had
the refund and her guests had their evening. What Maya wanted was to know
whether Maren would hire her to do anything again. It wasn’t a question she
wanted Maren to answer on a Monday morning. She crossed it off. She sent a
copy of the picture of Maren’s group at their table, which Maren had asked
for.

As she was leaving, her phone rang. A woman named Anika, who organized the
spring school auction, had heard they’d used Weller. She wanted to put a place
on the sponsor letter before the committee sent it around. Could Maya confirm
they could use the hall?

The lot was full of white lines from the frost that had stayed on the ground
in the shade. Maya walked to the car and unlocked it. She’d looked at Weller’s
calendar when she booked the supper. There had been a hole in March. She could
see the month in her mind, Saturday at the far right. The cell had looked
empty.

“What are you putting in the room?” she asked.

“Auction tables and maybe a dance floor. Two hundred people?”

“Will they eat at tables?”

“I have to ask. They did last time.”

“Okay. Send me the date and tell me what you want the room to do, even if it’s
two versions. I’ll ask Jo what she has. I can give you a status by two
tomorrow. Will that work for your letter?”

“They wanted me to send it this afternoon.”

“Can they send it with the date and leave the place open?”

There was a pause. Maya heard someone speak to Anika where she was.

“Yes,” Anika said. “I’ll tell them to leave that line out.”

Maya stayed in the car until she had put the call in her notebook. Anika would
send the date by the end of the day. Maya put a check in for ten the next
morning, early enough to call Anika and still get hold of Jo. She put two in
the calendar. Then she drove to the school. The side door was locked that day.
Nina was at the front, her cello case upright beside her. She’d sent a message
saying so. Maya had seen it before she left the lot.

After dinner, Nina set the music on the stand and pulled a pencil out of the
pocket of her case. She handed it to Maya.

“Will you listen to the beginning?” she said. “Mark where I come in.”

Maya was sitting close enough to see where Nina’s teacher had erased a
fingering. The paper was thinner there. She took the pencil and said yes. Nina
didn’t look back to see if she’d put the phone away. She raised her bow and
waited through the first four beats. Then four more.

Maya made a small mark beside the note.

* What Maya has just learned

The invoices go into a labelled envelope. Maya crosses off =call Maren=: what
she wanted from the call was reassurance, and that was never an action. Then a
stranger phones to ask whether she can confirm the same hall for the spring.
Maya can see the empty Saturday in her memory, and she doesn't answer from it.
She asks what the room has to do, says "I can give you a status by two
tomorrow", and stays in the car until the call is in her notebook and two
checks are on the calendar.

* Your trusted system — Step 17: Maintain

/Ten minutes, then a little every week./

A system built in a crisis usually stops being used when the crisis ends. Four
habits keep it going.

1. *Capture at the moment of the promise.* Before you leave the car, the room
   or the call, write it down. This is step 1 again, as a reflex.
2. *Answer with a time when you don't have the fact.* Say "I'll tell you by
   two tomorrow", then put both the check and the reply on your calendar. Don't
   confirm anything from memory.
3. *File what is finished.* Label it so you can find it next year, and take it
   off your lists.
4. *Review every week.* Take the time you measured in step 11, pick a day and
   an hour, and put it on the calendar as a repeating appointment.

Last, read your lists once more and cross off anything that is a wish. You
can only do actions.

You now have one inbox, six lists, a calendar and a review. Read the story's
first chapter again and count how many of them would have caught Tuesday.

- The reflex :: ~C-c r C~ captures and clarifies in one motion. For the call
  in the story it is used three times: =Anika — date and room requirements=
  as =delegate=; =Ask Jo what Weller has in March= as =next-action= with
  =@calls=; and =Give Anika a status= as =calendar= at =tomorrow 14:00=. If
  there is no time even for that, ~C-c r c~ takes one line and asks nothing.
- The weekly review :: open =gtd/calendar.org= and add a heading =Weekly
  review= (~M-RET~). Press ~C-c C-s~ and give the day and time. Then edit the
  timestamp it inserts so that it ends in =+1w=, for example
  =<2026-11-13 Fri 16:00 +1w>=. It now appears in the agenda every week. When
  it comes up, ~C-c r w~ opens that week's checklist, and marking the entry
  DONE moves it to the following week.
- Filing :: clarify finished paperwork as =reference= (~C-c r x~), or import
  the file itself with ~C-c r m~. ~C-c r /~ with =OR_TYPE= and =reference=
  finds it later.
- Wishes :: for an entry that was never an action, ~C-c C-t~ and CANCELLED,
  with a word about what you really wanted.

Then make the dashboard (~C-c r d~) the first thing you open each morning.
When it shows no unclarified captures, no stalled projects and no nameless
waits, the system is working.
