* The chapter

On Sunday morning Cal came for the dress in the bag under Maya’s bed. It had
belonged to his sister before Nina wore it. His sister wanted it for a school
play. He came upstairs carrying an empty bakery box to put it in, because Nina
had told him about the beads.

“Did I say I’d bring it?” Maya asked him.

“You said you’d look for it.”

The dress was where it had been the whole time. Maya had left the bag open;
the cat from upstairs, who came in whenever the front door was left ajar, had
slept on the tissue. Cal held up the dress by its straps. They picked hairs
off it together while Nina sat on the sofa with her music.

“I can get you Thursday if the fitting runs late,” Cal said.

“The fitting’s at four. I’m coming.”

“Okay. I’ll do warm-up.” He set the dress in the box. “I’ll tell her once. You
tell her if it changes.”

Maya had set aside Sunday morning to go through the notes she hadn’t gotten to
on Friday. She could see the note in the calendar on her phone. Before she
could turn it to silent, a message from Leila appeared. Leila had carried
plates for the last two suppers. This year she’d offered to run the side door.
Could she come in for the second dinner only? Her son’s futsal team had made
the semifinals. She wrote that she could take somebody’s place at the end and
help stack.

/Of course/, Maya wrote back. /See you for the late one./

She set the phone on the kitchen table. There was a cup of coffee on one side
of it and the notebook on the other. She took the rubber band off the
notebook. The envelope slid halfway out. She had scarcely pulled it free when
the phone lit up again.

/Are you staffing the ramp?/ Eli wrote.

She called him.

“I sent the shift sheet last night,” he said. “Leila just forwarded your text.
I had her from four thirty because she knows where the pantry guests get
dropped off. Now I’ve got nobody till six thirty.”

“Could you ask Ben to switch?”

“Ben’s driving the van. I already asked him.”

“I can stand at the door.”

“Where were you before you stood at the door?”

Maya looked at the cup on the table. She was supposed to be at check-in when
the first guests arrived. She could picture moving the check-in table close to
the ramp. Then guests who came to the front would have to walk around the
building. She’d need somebody there to tell them.

“Did Leila say when she could get there?” Eli asked.

“Just the second sitting.”

“He plays over on Grant, right? She might make six. I could keep Ben on the
door after he drops the van and give the keys to Luis.” Eli stopped. “Would
you be all right if I called her and worked this out?”

“Of course.”

“Can I give her a shift? Or do I need to check it with you?”

Maya heard Nina turn a page. “Give her a shift.”

“Last time you moved the load-in, Luis spent an hour at the loading dock
without anybody to let him in.”

“I remember the loading dock.”

“He was on my call sheet. He went there because it said to go there. I paid
him out of my pocket.”

Maya had known Luis was early. She hadn’t known what Eli had paid him. “Tell
me how much,” she said.

“I will. Today I need to know whose sheet it is.”

She was about to tell him the staffing sheet was his. That was what she had
told him last time. She put her finger on Leila’s name in the notebook, where
it had stayed since Thursday.

“Can you send me the sheet you gave the crew?” she said.

He sent it. Nina raised her bow and drew a note. The vibration reached the
kitchen table. Maya pulled the shift sheet up on the laptop. Eli had every
hand accounted for. Between four and five there were five separate jobs she’d
thought of as /set up/. She could see the window he was talking about, where
Ben could move to the ramp and Luis could finish with the van.

“Put the new times in your sheet,” she said. “I’ll tell Leila to get the time
from you. Anyone else asks me about a shift, I send them to you.”

She wrote to Leila again. It took longer to get the message right than it had
to give away the hour. When she looked up, Nina was fitting her endpin into
the hole in the rubber stop. She had been practicing with a pencil tucked
behind her ear.

“Want me to count?” Maya asked.

“I have the metronome,” Nina said. She tapped the screen of the little black
machine on her stand. It ticked four times before she put her bow down.

* What Maya has just learned

Maya answers a volunteer's text with "Of course", and a shift Eli had already
staffed comes apart. Eli asks, "I need to know whose sheet it is." Maya tells
the volunteer to get her time from Eli. Writing that message takes longer than
giving the hour away did.

* Your trusted system — Step 8: Owners

/Five minutes./

Every list and every decision has one owner. When you answer a request that
belongs on someone else's list, they are left to deal with what you promised.

1. Read down your Projects and Waiting For lists. Beside each entry that
   involves other people, write who decides. If it is you, write "me". If you
   don't know, finding out is a next action.
2. Start a page for each person you deal with often, titled with their name.
   When something comes up that is theirs to decide or that you need to raise
   with them, write it there. Don't answer it yourself.
3. Think of the last quick favor you granted. Was it yours to grant? If not,
   tell the owner today what you said.

From now on, a request that belongs to someone else gets one reply: "Ask
Eli. That's his sheet."

* The same step in Org Reason

- A point to raise with a person :: ~C-c r C~, type the point starting with
  their name (=Eli: who takes the van keys?=), choose =agenda=, and give the
  name again at =Who / meeting=. It is filed in =gtd/agendas.org= with the
  person recorded. Use a meeting's name in the same way for things to bring to
  a regular meeting.
- Finding everything for one person before you talk :: open =gtd/agendas.org=
  in your Org Reason directory and search for the name (~C-s~). Search
  =gtd/waiting.org= the same way to see what that person owes you. Starting
  each line with the name, as Maya does, is what makes this quick.
- Recording who decides on a project :: open the project from ~C-c r a~ and add
  a line under the heading, such as =Decides: Eli (staffing sheet)=.
- When you have handed a decision to someone and need to hear the result ::
  it is a WAIT entry, as in step 5.

- [ ] Each project that involves other people names who decides.
- [ ] At least one person has entries waiting for your next conversation.

* Carry forward

An owner on every shared commitment, and a list per person. Before your next
conversation with any of them, read their list.
