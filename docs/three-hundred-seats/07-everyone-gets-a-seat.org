* The chapter

Eli came back in from calling the caterer with his hands in his pockets. It
was cold in the lot and he’d gone out without his coat.

“Sergio says he can feed them in two groups,” he said.

Maya looked at Ruth. Ruth was reading a fire notice on the wall, close enough
to the glass that her breath showed on it.

“He can feed them how far apart?” Ruth asked.

“He needs to talk to the people who’d work it. I asked for a price. He says
Monday.”

“We need the room before Monday,” Maya said.

Jo looked up from her booking sheet. “I can move you to nine thirty for the
same price if you don’t need my bar staff. Your caterer does drinks, you stack
the chairs, you hand me an empty room at nine thirty.”

“Same as Merritt?” Ruth said to Maya.

Maya showed her the line for the room in the budget. Jo’s price was the same
down to the dollar. Maya had told her what they could spend, and Jo had quoted
that amount.

Jo went to get the contract. Ruth folded up her glasses.

“How much is it to walk away from this room?” she asked.

“Half to book. We lose the half if we walk.”

“And from Merritt?”

“Nothing. We never put money down.”

Ruth laid her glasses on the table. “So when you say we’ll serve them twice,
the thing you can buy before noon is the room.”

“Yes.”

“You can tell the board that.”

Maya drew the two circles on the back of the plan a second time, in the
margins of her notebook. The first time she had drawn them it had felt like
solving a puzzle. In the notebook they looked like two cups she had to fill.
She put the pencil across the page.

“What if we just tell them we’ve moved and we’ll send the times Monday?” she
said.

“They’ve already got the address. Tell them what you actually need from them.”

At eleven twenty-eight Maya called Doris. She described the ramp, starting
with which end of the parking lot to use. Doris asked whether her brother
could wait there while she went in.

Maya looked out the window. A white van stood beside the ramp with its
flashers on. Jo told her they’d mark one place for drop-offs and keep the rest
clear. Maya told Doris where her brother could stop and where he could park.
Doris asked her to hold on while she found something to write with. Maya
listened to two drawers open and close.

“You still need me at six?” Doris asked when she came back.

“We’re looking at an early and a late dinner. I don’t have your time yet.”

“Ask me for the early one, will you? Lenny’s up at four for work.”

Maya wrote /Doris — early if possible/ in the margin. Jo set the contract down
in front of her and slid it around so Maya could read it. The end time was in
a box by itself. Maya asked Jo to initial the line where she’d changed eight
thirty to nine thirty. Jo did. At eleven forty-six Maya signed. She waited
beside the table while Ruth called Alan with the news, listening for which
parts Ruth chose to tell him. Ruth used the word /booked/. When Alan asked
about dinner she said they were pricing a second service.

In the old bank email there was a link for lost tickets. Denise changed it to
a form where a guest could tell the team how many people were coming with them
and which of two dinner times would work. Maya wanted the subject line to say
/Everyone gets a seat/. Denise suggested /Please tell us which time you can
come/. They used Denise’s. The proposed times were five and seven. The form
said a reply was a preference, and that the team would confirm each party’s
time. By two, somebody had forwarded the email to the pantry with /Five for
me/ typed above it. The woman at the pantry desk called to ask whether that
meant five people or five o’clock.

Eli set up one guest list and put Doris on it. There was a place for her
reply, a place for the time she’d actually been given, and a space for a note
about the door. He left the middle column empty.

“What do you want me to do with the other replies?” he asked.

“Put them there too. I’ll check the email.”

“And Sergio?”

Maya opened the notebook. She had drawn a line through /Find a room/. Under it
she’d written /Serve both groups dinner/. It seemed like the right name for
the work she had left. She wrote /Sergio — cost and crew for two dinners,
Monday/ on the facing page and put the original Saturday load-in drawing
behind the signed agreement. There was a drawing for the old room, one for the
new room, and a sheet with two circles where a dinner ought to go.

On her calendar she held half an hour before pickup to look through the pages
she’d started that week. Her phone lit up two minutes before the half hour
began. A table host wanted to know whether she should get her party to fill
out the form separately. Maya typed a quick answer. Before she sent it, she
realized that if ten people filled it out separately, Eli would have to put
the table together again by name.

She called the host and sorted it out. When she hung up there was time to
review the notes or get to the side door at three thirty. She closed the
notebook, put it in her bag and drove to the school.

* What Maya has just learned

Before noon Maya can buy one thing, the room, so she signs for it and leaves
the price of a second service open until Monday. She draws a line through
=Find a room= and writes the work that is left: =Serve both groups dinner=.
She blocks half an hour to look through her week's pages. A phone call takes
it.

* Your trusted system — Step 7: Calendar

/Ten minutes./

The calendar holds only what has to happen on a certain day or at a certain
time. If you also fill it with things you merely hope to do, you stop
believing it.

1. Go through your captured list, Projects and Waiting For. Move onto the
   calendar every appointment, every deadline someone else has set, and every
   time you have promised an answer ("I'll call you by lunchtime").
2. Take off the calendar anything that isn't tied to its date. Those are next
   actions. Maya's coffee order sat at six o'clock for no reason she could
   remember.
3. When a project's outcome is reached, cross it off the Projects list. If
   work remains, give that work its own name as a new project.
4. Block thirty minutes this week to look over all your lists. Treat it as an
   appointment with someone you would not cancel on.

* The same step in Org Reason

Your appointments stay in whatever calendar you already use. Org Reason adds
dated commitments to its own agenda.

- A commitment for a day or time :: ~C-c r x~ on the inbox item, choose
  =calendar=, then give the date, with a time if there is one
  (=2026-11-14 11:30=). It moves to =gtd/calendar.org=.
- Something to bring back later :: choose =tickler= and a date. On that day
  the agenda shows it again. A tickler is a reminder to reconsider, with no
  commitment yet.
- A real deadline on a project or action :: ~C-c C-d~ on its heading.
- A finished project :: ~C-c C-t~ on the project heading, choose DONE. Capture
  the remaining work with ~C-c r C~ and clarify it as a new =project=.
- The review block :: ~C-c r C~, type =Look over all my lists=, choose
  =calendar=, give the day and time.

Ordinary next actions get no date. The agenda's first block then shows only
what truly belongs to each day.

- [ ] ~C-c r a~ shows this week with only real commitments on it.
- [ ] The inbox (~C-c r i~) is empty, or close to it, for the first time.

* Carry forward

A calendar you can believe, an empty inbox, and a half-hour appointment with
your lists. If the appointment gets eaten, as Maya's did, move it to a new
time straight away so it doesn't just disappear.
