* The chapter

At eight the next morning, Maya got into a gym with four hundred bleacher
seats and nowhere to put a pot of soup.

The woman whose husband had answered the phone stood beside a trophy case with
her arms tucked into her sweatshirt. Her name was Pearl. She pointed out a
door beside the basketball hoops.

“You could bring it in hot,” she said. “They did that at the wrestling
banquet.”

“How far is it from where we can plug in the warmers?”

“Oh, you can’t use those. We had a little fire in March.”

Eli walked in wearing a black knit cap and carrying a cardboard tube. He
listened to Pearl explain the fire. Then he walked from the door to the place
where the farthest table would go and back again. His shoes squeaked on the
floor.

“What did they serve at the wrestling banquet?” he asked.

“Pizza.”

In the parking lot Maya opened the notebook to the envelope she’d stuck
inside. Under /room/ she’d written the names of nine places. She put a line
through Pearl’s.

“How many of those can give us three hundred at tables?” Eli asked.

“The museum says four hundred.”

“Standing in the gallery. They put it on the website right under the picture.”

“They might have another room.”

“Call if you like.” Eli turned the cap of the cardboard tube. “What do you ask
them?”

“I ask if they have the twenty-first.”

“If they say yes?”

Maya rested the notebook on the roof of the car. A bus came down the street
with its lights still on. She wrote /300 at dinner tables/. Beside it Eli drew
a little rectangle for the stage. He asked where the food came out and what a
guest did if she couldn’t use steps. Maya remembered Mrs. Kline taking the
pantry stairs sideways. She added /step-free way in/. The space left under
/room/ was hardly the width of her hand.

Her first call after that lasted forty seconds. The museum could put a hundred
and forty at tables. Her second place could do two hundred if it moved the bar
outside. It was the wrong month for an outside bar. For each call she could
hear the person on the other end pause at a different word.

Eli was sliding the tube into the back of his car when Maya remembered the
license.

“Did you send it?”

“Monday. I sent it to the address you gave me.”

“Have they said we have the van?”

Eli found the sent message. There was no reply. The rental desk’s original
offer said they’d hold the van until ten Thursday. Maya looked at the
dashboard in Eli’s car. Nine twenty-one. On the envelope, /van/ looked just as
it had the night before. Eli had taken a picture of his license, found the
number and sent it off. A check mark beside /van/ could have meant he had done
everything Maya asked. They still couldn’t go anywhere in it.

She called the desk. The clerk put her on hold. A recording told her how easy
it was to reserve online.

“Why don’t you call me from the pantry when you hear?” Eli said.

Maya nodded, hung up and wrote, /Rental desk: confirm cargo van for Sat. Eli
sent license Mon. Call again 9:40 if no word./ On her way to the pantry she
stopped at a red light and remembered that she had to tell the building
manager about the freezer switch. For once the other thing, the thing she’d
been afraid of forgetting, didn’t follow it in.

At nine forty she was in the pantry office looking at an old invoice with
Weller Hall in the address line. She phoned the rental desk again. A different
clerk looked in the account, found Eli’s license attached to a message nobody
had opened and said a customer had just come in asking for a van that size.

“Is that our van?” Maya said.

“It is if you want it.”

She asked him to send the confirmation while she was on the line. She watched
it arrive, opened it, checked the date, and sent the pickup time to Eli. Then
she drew a line through the entry on the envelope. This time she knew what the
line meant.

The Weller invoice was for a memorial reception Maya had sent a crew to years
earlier. She hadn’t seen the room. The family had brought in a hundred and
twenty folding chairs and a portrait on an easel. Maya looked up the current
rental page. /Up to 300 guests./ There was no figure for dinner.

“Saturday the twenty-first?” said Jo, who ran the hall. “What happened to the
place you had?”

Maya looked at the names she’d crossed out. It was the first time someone had
asked what she’d already had, as if no one went looking for a room ten days
out unless they’d lost one.

“I missed a deadline.”

“Well. I have a Saturday with a hole in it. How late?”

“The last guest is supposed to leave at eight thirty. We need time to load
out.”

“Send me what you’re putting in the room. If it fits, we can talk about when
it comes out.”

Maya asked Eli how soon he could finish the drawing. He emailed it to her by
eleven. At eleven fifteen she had another hall on the phone that said it might
be able to move a dance lesson. Then Alan, the pantry board chair, called.
Maya went into the hall to take that one. When she came back, the drawing was
open under three other windows. She meant to send it as soon as she could tell
Jo whether they needed the bar as well as the buffet. At noon she went to find
Ruth.

* What Maya has just learned

=room= becomes =300 at dinner tables, step-free way in=, and nine candidate
halls shrink to a few in two phone calls. =van= becomes =Rental desk: confirm
cargo van for Sat. Call again 9:40 if no word=. When Maya finally draws a line
through it, she knows what the line means.

* Your trusted system — Step 3: Clarify

/Ten minutes./

Take the first ten lines of your captured list, in order. Don't skip the ugly
ones. For each, ask two questions.

1. *What would be true when this is finished?* Write a result someone else
   could check.
2. *What is the very next thing I would physically do?* Start with a verb:
   call, send, look up, draft. If you can't picture your hands doing it, it is
   still too big.

Then sort each line one of four ways:

- It takes under two minutes: do it now.
- It is one step: write the step on a new list titled *Next Actions*.
- It needs nothing done: keep it as reference, or cross it out.
- It needs several steps, someone else, or a particular day: put a mark beside
  it and leave it. Steps 4, 5 and 7 deal with those.

* Carry forward

A Next Actions list, and an inbox that still holds the bigger items. Clarify a
few more lines whenever you have five minutes. The next four steps empty the
rest.
