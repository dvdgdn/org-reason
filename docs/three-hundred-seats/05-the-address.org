* The chapter

At three fifteen the printer sent Maya a proof with the address of a building
she no longer had.

She had looked at that address on invoices, insurance forms and the back of
three hundred tickets. For a second it was a relief to see it in print. All
she had to do was check the spelling of Merritt Avenue and click the blue
button.

Then she saw the note from Denise at the top of the proof. /Need the new
address by 3:25. Cards and ticket email go from this file./

The office she was borrowing at the pantry shared a wall with the walk-in
freezer. When the compressor shut off she could hear Ruth in the next room
counting cases of beans. Ruth counted everything twice. At the end of the
summer, when Maya had told her they could raise fifteen thousand dollars in
one night, Ruth had opened the delivery schedule and started naming weeks.

Maya minimized the proof. Behind it was the floor plan Eli had drawn, with
round tables in staggered rows and a low stage against the back wall. He’d
marked the buffet tables in yellow because the last crew had mistaken them for
the bar and spent half an hour moving tubs of ice. He’d left room for Ruth to
get from the kitchen to the microphone without crossing in front of the
guests.

Her phone lit up.

/Side door today. I have the cello./

Nina had begun adding the pickup door to her messages after the day Maya had
waited on the wrong side of the building. By the time they’d found each other,
Nina had phoned her father and arranged a ride for herself. Maya typed /Side
door at three thirty/ and set the phone where she could see it.

On the pad beside her laptop she had written VENUE in a box. She had spent
most of the day making calls she could cross off and returning to a box that
looked exactly the same. Under the pad was a printout from Weller Hall. The
photograph showed a room with brick walls, high windows and somebody’s wedding
cake. /Up to 300 guests/, it said. There was an opening on the right Saturday.
Jo, who ran the hall, had written back while Maya was on another call.

/Send me the setup you want, including the stage and serving tables. Then I
can tell you how many fit. I’ve got someone else asking about the date./

Maya looked from the email to the drawing. She had been trying to think of
another hall so she could call Ruth with a decision. She had the drawing open
in front of her.

She put her pencil inside the box and wrote /Send Jo the dinner plan/. She
attached the drawing and asked Jo two things. How many could they seat with
this setup? Could Jo hold Saturday while she checked?

The email went. Maya drew one line through /Send Jo the dinner plan/. She had
her pencil halfway across VENUE before she lifted it.

Jo’s reply came while Maya was putting her charger in her bag. /Got the plan.
I’ll check it first thing. Saturday is yours to take until noon tomorrow./

The hold had a time on it. That felt like something she could tell Ruth. On
the facing page, below an entry about a van key, she wrote Jo — dinner
capacity. She put a reminder at nine thirty the next morning to call if Jo’s
answer hadn’t arrived. With eleven minutes to pickup, she zipped the bag.

Denise called as Maya stood up.

“The printer’s waiting on me. Do you have an address?”

“I have Weller on hold. Jo’s looking at the setup in the morning.”

“We can pull the card. I can get you a second run tomorrow. Six-forty with the
rush charge and the extra mailing.”

Maya opened the proof again. The bank was paying for the card to go in a
mailing it was already doing. The second run would come out of the benefit.
Ruth had shown her the budget earlier, with a finger planted on the difference
between what the dinner should raise and what the pantry needed for the
deliveries it had scheduled. Fifteen hundred dollars.

“Can you give me an hour?”

“I have six minutes. I can tell the board we’re waiting if that’s where we
are.”

Maya could picture the bank’s meeting room. Denise would be standing by the
screen while somebody asked how the first hall had been lost. Maya had heard
Ruth field the same question that morning. /We missed the confirmation/, Ruth
had said, with the /we/ doing a great deal of work.

From the next room came the roll of a pallet jack and the clap of the freezer
door. Maya looked at the photo again. There were tables in it, with flowers on
them and enough room between them for a woman in a blue dress to stand with a
glass in her hand. Maya enlarged the photograph. She could see the back of the
room and part of a serving table. She had spent a day looking for a room this
close to the pantry with a Saturday free.

“Use Weller,” she said. “Eighteen Dock Street.”

“For the email too?”

Maya looked at what she’d written on the facing page. Jo’s name was there, in
pencil. She could call in the morning.

“Yes,” she said. “You can send it.”

* What Maya has just learned

Maya sends Jo the drawing and crosses off the action. She stops her pencil
halfway across =VENUE=: the outcome now depends on Jo. She writes =Jo — dinner
capacity= and sets a reminder for nine thirty. Then a printer's deadline
arrives, and she announces the address before Jo has answered.

* Your trusted system — Step 5: Waiting For

/Five minutes./

Sending something finishes your action. The result is still open until the
other person answers.

Start a list titled *Waiting For*. Look through your projects, your sent
messages and what is left of your captured list. Find every result that now
depends on someone else's reply. For each write:

- the person's name;
- exactly what you are waiting for;
- the date you asked;
- *when you will check* if you have heard nothing, set early enough that you
  could still do something else.

Then read down the list and ask of each line: have I told anyone this is
settled? If you have, either tell them it is still open or move your check
time earlier. A hold with a time on it is not yet a yes.

* The same step in Org Reason

For an inbox item that is in someone else's hands: ~C-c r x~, choose
=delegate=, and at =Who / meeting= give the person's name. The item moves to
=gtd/waiting.org= as WAIT, with the person and today's date recorded.

For something not yet captured, ~C-c r C~ captures and clarifies in one go.
Write the line the way Maya does: =Jo — dinner capacity for our layout=.

To set the check time, open the entry from the agenda (~C-c r a~, ~RET~), press
~C-c C-s~ on its heading and give a date, or a date and time such as
=tomorrow 9:30=. It appears in the agenda on that day, still marked WAIT.

Org Reason does not send the request or the follow-up. It records that you are
waiting.

- [ ] The WAIT block in ~C-c r a~ shows a name on every line.
- [ ] ~C-c r d~ lists nothing under "Waiting entries missing a person".

* Carry forward

A Waiting For list with a check time on each line. Keep it beside Next
Actions. Any line you have already reported to someone as settled gets
checked first.
