* The chapter

Ten days before the Winter Supper, Maya Torres found out she had sold three
hundred tickets to a room she didn’t have.

The email arrived just after eight on Wednesday morning. She had cleared
everything else out of her inbox. She knew this because she’d been waiting to
see the little picture the mail app put up when there was nothing left to
read. She’d got the picture, reached for her coffee and watched it disappear.

/Sorry we couldn’t make it work this year/, Gene at Merritt Hall had written.
/I released the date this morning./

She called him before she finished reading.

“Tell me released means you put it back on the calendar.”

“I put it back on the calendar.”

“So take it off.”

“I did. The union took it for their retirement dinner.”

Maya held the coffee away from her blouse. The lid had a crack in it and the
coffee came out warm against her thumb. “Gene. We have tickets.”

“I know. That’s why I gave you till Tuesday.”

“Did you send me that?”

“I told you when you called about the kitchen.”

She could see the kitchen at Merritt, the long steel counter by the back door
and the pipe that hung down just low enough for a tall man to hit his head.
She’d called Gene to ask if the new ovens had been installed. Nina had been in
the car with her, holding a paper bag of peaches in her lap. One had rolled
out under the seat.

Tuesday, Gene had said. I’ve got a man asking, and I can only keep telling him
no for so long.

She remembered saying, /I’ll get back to you by Tuesday./ She remembered Nina
stretching her hand under the seat and coming up with an earring Maya thought
she’d lost at a wedding.

“Can you ask the union if they’ll move?” she said.

“Their deposit’s in. I’ve given them the keys for a walkthrough.” He waited.
“I can give you the number of a place that does a good dinner for a hundred.”

After they hung up she searched Gene’s name anyway. There was an insurance
certificate from last year and a picture of a drain. She could account for
every email.

The tickets lay in bundles on the pantry office floor. The printer had put a
strip of blue paper around every twenty-five, a small flag sticking up from
each stack. Maya picked up a bundle and turned it over. Merritt Hall, 44
Merritt Avenue. On her way in, she had set a bundle aside for Ruth to give to
the board. She put it back with the others.

Ruth was outside with the Wednesday driver. They stood behind the van,
studying something on a clipboard. Maya waited for the driver to leave. He put
one finger on the sheet, said something, and Ruth moved his finger over one
line.

“Merritt gave away our Saturday,” Maya said.

Ruth passed the clipboard through the van window. “Go to Chalmers first. Mrs.
Kline’s ride comes at ten.” She put her hand on the sill and kept it there
until the van started. Then she turned to Maya. “What happened?”

“I missed the confirmation.”

“Where are we in the booking?”

“There’s no deposit to lose.”

“I’m asking if there’s a room to get back.”

Maya looked at the gate the van had gone through. “No.”

They walked into the packing room. A woman in a wool cap was cutting apart the
cardboard trays the cans came in. On a folding table lay the white bags Ruth
had asked the volunteers to pack first. They had numbers on them in marker.
Maya had assumed the numbers were for inventory. Ruth put a can of tomatoes
into bag six.

“Will you have to cancel these?” Maya asked.

“Today? No. These are paid for.” Ruth reached for another can. “The supper
covers the winter runs. Fourteen thousand dollars. If I can’t tell the drivers
what weeks they’re working, some will take other work. Then we can have a
freezer full of food and nobody to bring it to Chalmers.”

Maya remembered the night Ruth had asked her to take the event. They had
walked through Merritt together after a wedding. There was cake on the carpet.
Ruth had stepped around it and asked how many people they’d need to sell a
dinner to. Maya had counted the tables and said she could fill the room. Ruth
had been the only person to ask how she knew. Maya had shown her the list of
people who’d bought tickets to the smaller dinner the year before. Most of
those people had bought again.

“Give me today,” Maya said.

“I can give you till noon Friday. That’s my board call. I need to tell them we
have a room, or I need to tell them what it costs to cancel.” Ruth opened a
bag that had folded shut. “And Maya? Tell Eli now.”

Maya had planned to tell Eli when she could tell him the new address. She had
already begun, in her head, the call in which they both got to laugh about how
close they’d come.

She took the blue band off a bundle. There were still twenty-five tickets
inside. She opened her notebook and wrote /Friday, noon. Ruth needs a room or
the cost to cancel/. Then she dialed Eli.

“I need you to stop work on the Merritt drawing,” she said when he picked up.

His end went quiet. She could hear paper moving.

“How much of it?” he asked.

“All the part that’s Merritt.”

She heard him set down what must have been a ruler. “Okay,” he said. “Tell me
what I can keep.”

* What Maya has just learned

Maya's email inbox is empty and she still loses the hall. The promise that
mattered, "I'll get back to you by Tuesday", was made on the phone in a car.
It never reached anything she would look at again.

* Your trusted system — Step 1: Notice where promises arrive

/Five minutes./

An empty inbox only proves that one entrance is clear. Commitments also arrive
in calls, hallways, texts, voicemail, paper and your own head.

1. List every place a promise can reach you. Include the spoken ones.
2. Think back over the last week. Find one thing you said yes to out loud that
   is written down nowhere. Write it down now, in whatever words come.
3. Choose one place where new commitments will go from today: a notebook, an
   app, a tray. One. It has to be with you when people ask you for things.

You don't have to do anything about what you wrote. For now it only has to
be somewhere other than your memory.

* The same step in Org Reason

Your one place is the Org Reason inbox.

1. Press ~C-c r c~. At the prompt, type the spoken promise from step 2 above
   and press ~RET~. Nothing else is asked: no project, no date, no category.
2. Press ~C-c r c~ again and type your list of entrances as one line, for
   example =Promises reach me by: work email, phone calls, texts, the kitchen
   whiteboard, meetings=.
3. Press ~C-c r i~ to open the inbox. Both entries are there, each with an ID
   and the time you captured it.

- [ ] Two captures are in the inbox, and you decided nothing about either.

If you are often away from Emacs when promises are made, decide now how they
get to the inbox: a pocket notebook you empty each evening, or files dropped
into =inbox/drop/= and imported with ~C-c r I~.

* Carry forward

Your one capture place, and the list of entrances. Step 2 fills the first and
uses the second.
