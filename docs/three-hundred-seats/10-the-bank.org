* The chapter

At five forty on Monday, Denise let Maya into the bank through a door with no
handle on the outside. Half the lobby lights were off. The tellers’ chairs
were empty, but Maya could smell popcorn. Someone behind a partition was
listening to a basketball game on a radio.

“You can have some,” Denise said. “They make it when the branch closes.”

She took Maya into a room with a long table and a sink. A row of framed
photographs faced the table. At the far end was a photograph of last year’s
supper. The bank president had one arm around Ruth and was holding an
oversized check. Ruth had kept her apron on.

“I need thirty-three hundred more,” Maya said when they’d sat down. “That’s
what it takes to keep the dinner and pay for the deliveries.”

Denise pulled a folder toward her. In it was a copy of the bank’s agreement
with the pantry. Maya knew the first page. She’d put the sponsor logo at the
bottom of the tickets to match the one on the letter. On the second page she
saw a place for the bank to say where the money would come from. It came out
of a line with a number next to it. Denise covered the other lines with a
sheet of blank paper. There was a total for the year and a total already
committed. The two numbers were the same.

“If you ask me for another dollar,” Denise said, “I have to take it back to
the committee. They meet in December.”

“Didn’t Lewis say they wanted to be more involved?”

“He meant he wanted people to come.”

Maya looked at the photograph. She had planned to stand where Ruth was
standing in it, out of the way of the big check, with just enough time to find
everybody who belonged in the picture. She’d come to ask Denise for a number
and had caught herself picturing that moment as proof the number would be
there.

“Will the committee want to take back the five?” she asked.

“It’s for the winter deliveries. We’re still paying it.” Denise closed the
folder. “I stopped the envelopes, by the way. We pulled your cards. The bank
mailing went on time.”

“Who did that?”

“Me and whoever was in the break room when I asked.” Denise went over to the
sink and filled a glass. “It would have been easier to say the hall wasn’t
confirmed. I wish I’d had that to tell them.”

“You did,” Maya said. “I told you to send it anyway.”

Denise drank half the water and set the glass down. “Do you know when I first
saw you work?”

Maya shook her head.

“The library opening. The caterer wanted to load the truck and go. He said the
check was late and he wasn’t putting one tray down until he had it. You took
his call while you were on the ladder hanging the sign. You sent me to get
him. I was working in the library then.”

Maya remembered which ladder it was. An aluminum one with a loose foot. For
years she had told that story starting with the ring of the phone. By the time
she’d come down, the caterer was waiting by the ladder with his jacket on.

“You told him exactly where the check was,” Denise said. “I remember that. In
the librarian’s bottom drawer with the stamps. You got her to open the drawer
in front of him.”

“She’d had it the whole time,” Maya said.

“He didn’t have it.”

From behind the partition somebody groaned. A ball had hit a rim, twice, and
come out. Denise looked at the clock.

“I can put people on the phones,” she said. “There are four of us here who
know how to reach the people who won’t see an email. Some of the older guests
bought at the branch. I have those numbers with their tickets.”

“For what? Confirming times?”

“For whatever you decide you can offer. Give us one sheet so we all tell them
the same thing.”

Maya took out her notebook. /Denise — four callers once we have a script./ She
wrote it in front of Denise so Denise could see it.

“Is that enough?” Denise asked.

“It’s something I can use.”

Denise held the door for her. Outside, a man was trying the cash machine with
gloves on. He took one glove off with his teeth. Maya dialed Ruth from the
sidewalk. She’d wanted to make this call with good news. She watched the man
pull his cash out of the slot and said, when Ruth picked up, “The bank’s five
is solid. There isn’t more.”

* What Maya has just learned

The bank has no more money this year; its committee meets in December. It
does have four people who can make phone calls, once there is one sheet
telling them all what to say. Maya writes =Denise — four callers once we have
a script= where Denise can see her write it, then phones Ruth with the news
she had hoped not to give.

* Your trusted system — Step 10: Not yet, and maybe

/Five minutes./

Your Next Actions list should hold only what you could do today. Other things
need a place too, kept off that list.

1. Read your Next Actions. Mark every line that can't start until something
   else happens. Move each one off the list and into its project's notes,
   written with its trigger: "Brief the callers /once the script exists/."
2. Start a list titled *Someday / Maybe*. Move onto it anything you would like
   to do but are not committing to now. It is fine for this list to be long.
3. When someone offers help you can't use yet, write down the offer, who made
   it and what it is waiting on. Let them see you write it.
4. If one of your waiting-fors has come back as a no, tell the person who is
   counting on it today. Say what is solid and what is not.

* The same step in Org Reason

- A project step that can't start yet :: open the project (~C-c r a~, ~RET~),
  add a heading inside it as in step 6, but with ~C-c C-t~ choose TODO, not
  NEXT. Put the trigger in the heading: =Brief the four callers (once the
  script exists)=. TODO steps stay with the project and are not offered as
  available work. When the trigger happens, ~C-c C-t~ changes it to NEXT.
- An action already on the list that turns out to be blocked :: open it and
  change NEXT to TODO the same way, or to WAIT if a person is the block.
- A someday item :: ~C-c r x~ in the inbox, or ~C-c r C~ for a new one, and
  choose =someday=. It goes to =gtd/someday.org= and appears in its own block
  at the bottom of the agenda.
- Something to reconsider on a date :: choose =tickler= and give the date,
  for example the day after the committee meets.

- [ ] Every NEXT in ~C-c r a~ is something you could start today.
- [ ] The SOMEDAY block has at least one thing in it that you are glad not to
  be doing this month.

* Carry forward

A Next Actions list with nothing blocked on it, later steps parked with their
triggers, and a Someday / Maybe list. You now have every list the system
needs. Step 11 looks at them all together.
