* The chapter

On Thursday at four, Maya met Eli at the pantry before she left for her
fitting. She brought the Weller contract and a copy of the drawing with
sixteen tables. The pianist would bring a keyboard. It had a stand. Jo had a
stool. Maya had written those things down because she had already asked each
question once.

“I could ask Jo to do it by video,” she said. “If you can walk the room, maybe
she can call me and put me on while you’re there.”

Eli pulled out a chair with the heel of his boot. “Who signs?”

“I’d sign it after. Or you could sign it if it was fine.”

“Who says it’s fine?”

She sat down in the chair he’d pulled out. On the table between them was the
plan with sixteen circles. Each circle had ten tiny lines. She’d spent an hour
putting in the lines after everyone else went home. From the corner where the
food would come in, a wide stripe ran to the side door. She hadn’t wanted to
cover the old drawing so she’d put the stripe in blue.

“Jo says it’s fine for her room,” Maya said. “You say if it works for us.”

“And if we move something?”

“Keep it at a hundred and sixty or fewer. Keep the lane from the ramp and the
kitchen open. We have two hundred left in the setup allowance for anything you
need to move or rent. You can use it without calling me. Sign with Jo if you
can make it work inside that. If you can’t, don’t sign. Tell me what we’d have
to change.”

He read the drawing from where he stood. “The bank asked Denise for its own
table.”

“They have ten tickets in the first group. We’ve got ten chairs for them.”

“What if they want the place by the door?”

“Offer them the ten seats we’ve got.”

“If she calls you?”

“I’ll tell her you’re signing.”

Eli turned the contract around and looked at the line with Maya’s name on it.
“Tell her before she calls.”

Maya sent Jo a note that Eli would approve and sign for the event. She copied
Denise and Ruth. She wrote what he could decide and asked them to bring
anything about the room to him before the walkthrough. Then she sent him the
drawing. He already had a paper copy but wanted the file, in case he had to
send out a different one.

As he packed the drawing into the tube, she asked about Luis.

“He’s on the van paperwork now. I called the desk while they had him there.”

“And the hour you paid him on the last job?”

Eli gave her the number. Maya wrote it in the notebook next to his name. “Send
me what you paid him,” she said. “I’ll put it through my company. You
shouldn’t have to carry it.”

He put his hand on the tube. It was the first time all afternoon he’d stopped
moving.

“Enjoy the recital,” he said.

At six, Jo and Eli set two tables in Weller the way the new drawing showed
them. Jo wanted to see how the door swung with the nearest table occupied. Eli
took the chair closest to it and sat. Jo opened the door and left it open. Eli
put his feet where he would have put them if he’d come in off his shift and
wanted to stretch his legs. He could still see the stripe of bare wood all the
way back to the kitchen.

Denise arrived in her bank clothes. She had a little frame in her hand with
the bank’s name printed on both sides. “Could we put a table here just for the
board?” she asked. She meant the open patch of floor near the side entrance.
“Lewis wants a shot with Ruth in front of the sponsor sign. We could leave the
board sitting and bring Ruth over.”

Eli walked to the spot and stood where the table’s nearest chair would be. Jo
was still holding the door. From here he could almost reach it with his hand.

“They can sit together over there,” he said. He pointed to the first table
behind the piano. “I’ll put the frame down before doors. You can bring them to
the sign when people are leaving.”

“Could you get a picture of ten people at that table?”

“From the piano, sure. If you want them behind the sign, stand them behind the
sign. We can put it by the front wall once the first group clears.”

Denise looked at the blue stripe on the page. “Is Maya still on her way?”

“She’s at the recital. I’m signing.”

Denise carried the frame across the floor and looked back toward the piano.
She held it at arm’s length to see where it would show in a picture. “Will you
mark this as ours?” she said.

Eli wrote /bank/ inside the circle. Jo signed below the new drawing. He took a
picture of it and sent it to the crew with the time they could start laying
tables on Saturday.

At the school, the first two rows of chairs had tape across the backs. On some
strips children had written names; on others they’d written /Mom/ or /Dad/.
Maya found her first and last name in Nina’s small square handwriting. The
woman in the next chair lifted a coat so Maya could get in.

Nina came out with three other children. She looked down the row until she
found Maya, lifted her chin and then looked at her stand. Maya had seen her do
that before when she was checking whether the music lay flat. She thought that
was what it was, until Nina did it again without looking down.

The teacher introduced the group. She moved a microphone that was picking up a
faint hum and tucked the wire under the edge of the mat. Maya put her phone in
her bag. Just before she did she saw that Eli had sent a picture. She couldn’t
make out what was in it. She put it away anyway.

Two violins began. Maya counted under her breath. Four and four. Then the
cello. At home Nina had stopped after the first few notes and gone back. Here
she kept going. Maya heard a place where the tune she knew went off into the
violins. She heard it come back to Nina three notes lower than it had left.
The child at the far end lost her place for half a bar and came in with such
assurance that a man across the aisle lowered his program and smiled.

Afterward, in the hall, Nina asked, “Did you hear the bit at the start?”

“I heard you come in,” Maya said.

“I thought I went too early.”

“I counted two, the way you showed me.”

Nina set the cello down on its side. “What did it sound like?”

Maya thought for a moment. “Like you’d been there the whole time. Like I’d
just noticed.”

Nina looked at her, trying this answer out. Then she handed Maya the music and
picked up the cello. She didn’t ask what was on the phone.

* What Maya has just learned

The walkthrough and the recital are at the same hour. Maya first offers to
join by video and sign afterwards. Eli asks, "Who says it's fine?" So she
gives him the decision: a hundred and sixty or fewer, the lane from the ramp
and the kitchen kept open, two hundred dollars he can spend without calling,
and don't sign if it can't be made to work. She tells the others before they
can ask her. Then she sits in the chair with her name on it and puts her phone
away.

* Your trusted system — Step 14: Delegate the decision as well

/Ten minutes./

If you hand over a task but keep the decision, you still have to be there.
Hand over both, and say where the edges are.

Choose one thing this week that someone else could decide. Write five lines.

1. *Outcome:* what has to be true at the end.
2. *Limits:* what must not change, in numbers where you can.
3. *Means:* the money, time or tools they may use without asking.
4. *Stop:* the point at which they do not go ahead, and what they bring back to
   you.
5. *Report:* what you want to hear afterwards, and when.

Give the five lines to the person. Then tell everyone else involved who decides
now. Do it before the moment arrives, so nobody phones you in the middle.

Put the report on your Waiting For list. Then be unavailable. If someone asks
you anyway, the answer is the person's name.

* The same step in Org Reason

1. Press ~C-c r C~ and type the outcome as one line, for example
   =Eli — signed final floor plan for Weller=. Choose =delegate= and give the
   name.
2. Open the entry from the agenda (~C-c r a~, ~RET~). Under the heading, write
   the other four lines:
   #+begin_example
   Limits: 160 or fewer per sitting; lane from ramp and kitchen stays open.
   Means: $200 setup allowance, no need to call.
   Stop: if it can't work inside that, don't sign; tell me what would change.
   Report: photo of the signed plan, Thursday evening.
   Told: Jo, Denise, Ruth — Thursday 4 pm.
   #+end_example
3. ~C-c C-s~ on the heading sets when you expect the report.
4. Send the brief yourself. The entry is your record of what you said.

When the report comes, read the =Limits= line against it before you mark the
entry DONE.

- [ ] One WAIT entry carries an outcome, limits, means, a stop rule and a
  report time.
- [ ] The =Told:= line names everyone who might otherwise have asked you.

* Carry forward

One decision that someone else now owns, and an hour of your own given back.
Notice what you did with the hour.
