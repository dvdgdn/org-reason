* The chapter

By Friday afternoon the four callers knew whose dog would bark when the phone
rang and whose machine cut them off at thirty seconds. Denise was putting
names to the voices on her sheet. At the desk next to hers, a man from the
bank read the words /light food/ slowly to someone. He asked if they wanted to
know what it was. Then he listened for a while and said a refund was fine.

Maya had already given Sergio the guaranteed count and the dietary requests.
On the shared sheet she could see the group at five and the group at seven
growing by ones and twos. Sometimes the number for a group went down. Each
change left a name behind. At twenty to three the list showed twenty-seven
refunds. There were six seats still open under Maren’s name. Four of her ten
guests had accepted seven o’clock.

Maya had written /27 + 6/ at the edge of her pad. She knew they could cover
six more. She had written it there before she knew whether there were other
calls still out. Now that Denise said they’d heard from everyone else, she put
a line through the sum. It was a number about the pantry. It couldn’t tell
Maren what to do with six seats.

At three Maya called. Maren picked up outside. A crossing signal counted down
behind her.

“I have two more who can come at seven,” she said. “The rest are going out
after their shift. Can you give their money back to them?”

“You paid us for the whole table,” Maya said. “I can send three hundred back
to your card. Then you can pay the four of them, if that works for you.”

“Did you say three hundred?”

“Four tickets at seventy-five.”

Maren was quiet for a few seconds. “I only took money from three. The other
woman’s the one who’s leaving. We were treating her.”

“Do you want me to go through the names with you?”

“Yes,” Maren said. “Please.”

They went through them. One woman had been down as /M. Hall/ and another as
/Marie H./ They turned out to be sisters with almost the same name. Both were
coming. Maya changed the initials on the list so the crew at the door could
tell them apart. Maren said she’d come a few minutes early; she had a card for
the woman who was leaving and wanted the others to sign it before they went
out.

“Can I bring it in?” she asked.

“Of course. There’s room at your table.”

The crossing signal started again. Maren said goodbye. Maya sent the
confirmation and checked that the refund had gone to the card Maren used. When
the receipt appeared she looked at the two numbers it gave her: the amount
returned and the amount still paid. She remembered how she used to look only
for the word /successful/.

At five, Denise called out the door counts. A hundred and forty-three at five.
A hundred and twenty-six at seven. Maya added them, then added thirty-one. The
list came back to three hundred.

Ruth was on the floor marking boxes with tape. Maya took her the pad instead
of calling across the room.

“Fourteen thousand three hundred seventy-five, if we come in on the quoted
costs,” Maya said.

“Say the number of tickets first.”

“Thirty-one refunds. We’ve sent them all.”

Ruth let the end of the tape curl around her finger. “Anybody we didn’t call?”

“Everybody had an answer.”

Ruth pressed the tape down with her thumbnail. “Good.” She had written two
versions of the notes for her board call. One was folded inside the other. She
took the inner one out and put it in the drawer where she kept the route
schedule. Then she took the other into the office and shut the door.

* What Maya has just learned

A table of ten turns out to be six arrivals and four refunds, and one of the
four was being treated by the others, so the money goes back to three people.
Maya goes through the names one by one. When the refund receipt appears she
reads both its numbers. She used to look only for the word "successful". The
two receptions and the refunds add up to three hundred. Ruth asks, "Anybody we
didn't call?"

* Your trusted system — Step 15: Close the loops

/Ten minutes./

Crossing a line off shows that you did something. It doesn't show that the
thing arrived, was the right amount, or reached the right person.

1. Take the last ten things you crossed off. For each, ask what you saw that
   proves the result: a reply, a receipt, the thing itself. Where you saw
   nothing, look now.
2. Take your Waiting For list. For each answer received, ask whether it
   answered your actual question or a neighbouring one. If you are unsure, go
   through the details with the person.
3. Pick one finished project and make it add up. Count what went in and what
   came out: invitations sent against replies received, money owed against
   money paid. The totals should match. Where they don't, something is still
   open.
4. Ask Ruth's question of the whole project: is there anybody we didn't
   answer?

Only then mark the project done.

* The same step in Org Reason

1. Open =gtd/actions.org= in your Org Reason directory, press ~C-c / T~ and
   type =DONE=. Org folds the file down to what you have completed. For any
   you can't vouch for, add a line under the heading saying what you saw:
   =Receipt: $300 returned, $450 still paid.= ~C-c C-c~ removes the
   highlighting afterwards.
2. If one turns out not to be finished, ~C-c C-t~ puts it back to NEXT or
   WAIT. Reopening is cheap and the entry keeps its history.
3. For the project you are reconciling, open its file and read every heading
   beneath it. Each should be DONE or CANCELLED, with nothing left as TODO.
   Then ~C-c C-t~ and DONE on the project heading itself.
4. ~C-c r A~ moves completed single actions to =archive/actions.org=. They
   keep their IDs, and ~C-c r /~ with =OR_TYPE= and =action= still finds them.

If you froze a forecast in step 12 and its outcome is now known, ~C-c r o~
records what happened beside what you predicted. The original wording stays
as it was.
