* In the story

On the back of an envelope Maya writes eleven things from her memory, her
pockets and her messages: =room=, =van=, =coffee=. The twelfth is a worry, not
a task. Writing it down doesn't mean she has to send it to anyone. Only then
can she hear her daughter's first note.

* Your trusted system — Step 2: Capture everything

/Fifteen minutes. This is one of the two long steps./

Take the list of entrances from step 1 and go through them one at a time:
messages, pockets, bag, desk, voicemail, then your head. Write down everything
that has your attention. Tasks, promises, unanswered messages, things to fix,
decisions, and worries with no action attached.

Three rules:

- One line per thing. A single word is enough if you will know what it means
  tomorrow.
- Don't sort, rank or solve. =van= is a complete entry today.
- Don't stop at the work things. The recital goes on the same list as the venue.

You are finished when you sit for a full minute and nothing new comes. Expect
thirty to a hundred lines. That number is normal.

* The same step in Org Reason

1. Press ~C-c r c~, type one thing, press ~RET~. Repeat. The prompt takes one
   line and files it at once, so the rhythm is fast.
2. For something already on screen in Emacs, select the text and press
   ~C-c r c~: the selection is captured without retyping.
3. For paper, a photograph or a voice memo, press ~C-c r m~ and choose the
   file. Org Reason keeps a copy of the original with an inbox entry pointing
   to it.
4. Capture the worries too. Later you may file one as a =feeling= or a
   =question=, which is not a task and never shows up as one.

Do not press ~C-c r x~ yet, however obvious an item looks.

- [ ] ~C-c r d~ opens the dashboard, and its first line counts your
  unclarified captures. Note the number.
