* The chapter

Maya was in the school drop-off lane when Jo’s answer came. Nina had just
wrestled the cello out of the back seat. Maya watched her stop to let a little
boy pass, then take the short steps up to the side door one at a time. The
crossing guard motioned for Maya to pull forward. She drove around the corner
and parked behind a delivery van.

/We can seat 220 with your dinner setup. That includes the stage and serving
tables on the drawing. For 300, I can set chairs in rows. I’ll hold the date
through noon as promised./

Maya read it twice. At two hundred twenty she was eighty seats short. She
brought up the guest list and scrolled until the row numbers caught up.
Somewhere around row two hundred twenty she was looking at the names of people
who had bought tables together.

She called Denise.

“Where are the cards?”

“With the mailroom. They go out at ten.”

“Can you get the boxes back?”

There was a little pause. “The email went yesterday, Maya.”

“I know. Get the boxes back if you can. I’ll call you at eleven.”

Maya was still in the car when a guest named Doris Heller called. She wanted
to know if the entrance on Dock Street had steps. Her brother was coming
straight from work and had offered to drop her by the door. She’d looked at
the picture online and could see a flight of steps at the front.

Maya opened her mouth to say the hall would have another entrance. She knew it
had a loading door. She had no idea what lay between that door and the room.

“I’m going to see it this morning,” she said. “Can I call you by lunchtime?”

“I leave at twelve thirty. Before then would be good.”

Maya wrote Doris’s number on the same page as Jo’s and put eleven thirty on
the calendar. Then she called Eli and asked him to bring the drawing. She told
Ruth the number before she told her what she planned to do about it. Ruth said
she’d meet them at Weller at ten.

The hall looked bigger from the doorway than it had in the picture. The
windows went up two stories. The building had once been a cable works, Jo
said. There was a square cut into the wood floor where machinery had stood.
Maya could see why people hired it for weddings. With the tables folded away
it would have taken a long time to walk across carrying a drink.

Jo took them to one of the round tables set up for a wedding that weekend. Eli
put his drawing on the cloth. Jo slid her own plan next to it. Twenty-two
circles. Maya went around them with her finger, counting a second time after
she’d reached the bottom.

“What if we take the stage away?” Maya asked.

“I can get you another table, maybe two. How many people do you need to seat?”

“Three hundred.”

Jo went to the stack of chairs against the wall. Together she and Maya carried
one over and put it at the table. There were already ten. Maya backed the new
chair into the space between the table and a little cart with tubs on it. The
chair fit if the person sitting in it drew their knees in. Eli took the cart
by the handle and tried to go past. The near wheel clipped a chair leg. He
took his hand off the cart.

“That’s the route from the kitchen,” Jo said. “It’s the way in from the ramp,
too.”

Maya looked down the length of the room to the door. In the photo she’d
studied yesterday the woman with the glass had been standing right about here.
She’d had the whole aisle to herself.

Jo showed her the ramp. It came in from the side lot on Dock Street and went
straight to a wide door. There was a metal lip at the threshold, low enough
for the cart to roll over it. Maya took a picture from the pavement so she
could tell Doris where to ask her brother to stop.

Ruth arrived in her pantry apron with her coat buttoned over it. She listened
to Jo, took a slow look around the room and asked Maya how quickly they could
tell the guests.

“We have it until nine thirty that night,” Maya said. “We could seat them in
two groups. One early, one late.”

Jo checked her booking sheet. “If you’re packed out by nine thirty. You’d need
to leave time to reset the room.”

Maya turned over the drawing. Eli held it flat while she traced the base of a
water glass twice. She wrote 150 in each circle.

“I can ask Sergio what he needs to do two services,” he said. “I’ll need times
to give him.”

Ruth looked at the second circle. “These people bought dinner, Maya. Some of
them bought a whole table.”

“We’ll keep parties together. We can ask who can come early.”

“And what am I telling my board at noon?”

Maya’s phone buzzed on the table. Denise had got the boxes back. A minute
later came a screenshot of the ticket email with Weller Hall under the words
/We look forward to seeing you/. Maya turned the phone so Ruth could see it.

“Tell them everyone gets a seat,” she said. “Two sittings.”

Eli folded the drawing between the two circles and went out to call the
caterer.

* What Maya has just learned

The brochure said "up to 300 guests". With Maya's tables, stage and serving
line, the room seats two hundred twenty. One extra chair blocks the cart and
the way in from the ramp. The number she had announced described a different
room from the one she needed.

* Your trusted system — Step 6: Outcomes you can check

/Ten minutes./

Open your Projects list and pick the three that matter most this month.

1. Rewrite each outcome so that a stranger could walk in and tell whether it
   is done. Include the conditions that would make a near miss useless: how
   many, by when, for whom, at what cost. "Hall booked" becomes "Hall booked:
   300 seated at dinner tables, step-free entrance, out by 9:30".
2. Under each, make two short columns: *Known* and *Assumed*. A fact goes under
   Known only if you have seen it yourself or someone responsible has told you.
   A brochure, a photograph and "it should be fine" go under Assumed.
3. For the assumption that would hurt most if it were wrong, write a next
   action that tests it. Put a chair at the table.

* The same step in Org Reason

1. Press ~C-c r a~ and ~RET~ on a project to open its file.
2. Edit the project heading itself until it states the checkable outcome. Its
   ID does not change, so links to it still work.
3. Under the heading, below the property drawer, add:
   #+begin_example
   Known:
   - Jo is holding Saturday until noon.
   Assumed:
   - The room seats 300 with our layout.
   #+end_example
4. Add the test as an action inside the project. Put point on the existing
   NEXT heading, press ~C-RET~ for a new heading at the same level, type the
   action, then ~C-c C-t~ and choose NEXT. ~C-c C-q~ adds a context tag.
5. Save with ~C-x C-s~.

Actions kept under their project are the ones the dashboard counts when it
checks that the project is moving.
