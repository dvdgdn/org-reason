* The chapter

On Tuesday Maya went to the pantry early, before the freezer started its
morning cycle. She put the notebook, the laptop and the envelope from her
kitchen table in front of her. Then she took a paper bag out of her coat
pocket. She’d used it in the car to write down two things while she waited for
Nina. One was /where does the van go after unload/. The other was /music
stand/.

She’d told Ruth on the phone that she’d bring her options. All she had so far
was a number and a small list of cuts she couldn’t make add up. She had twenty
minutes before Ruth got in and began to answer the guest replies. It would
have been satisfying to see the unread count go down. She moved the mail
window out of the way and opened the calendar instead.

There was the date of the supper, the load-in, and the nine-thirty finish.
Friday at noon said /final headcount to Sergio/. Thursday said /fitting/, then
/Nina, recital/. The week after the supper had a dentist appointment she’d
forgotten she’d moved. She checked the confirmation in her messages and
changed it. Below that was a reminder to call Gene on Friday and ask when they
could pick up the keys for a room they weren’t going to use. She deleted it.

She put the messages beside the notebook. The van was confirmed. Doris had
directions, but no time. Eli had put Leila at the late door and Ben at the
early door. Luis could take the keys if the rental desk added him to the
paperwork. Maya wrote /Ask Eli to confirm Luis can drive the rental/ under the
things she needed to discuss with him. She stopped herself from writing /Done/
beside Leila. The first shift was filled; the keys still had to change hands.
She made a place on the page for the names of people she was waiting for and
the dates they’d said they would answer. If there was no date, she picked a
time to check.

The paper bag went in a little stack with the envelope. She sent the building
manager the photograph of the freezer switch; it took less time than finding
the photograph. The coffee order was part of the food quote, so she crossed it
off. On a page near the back she wrote /ask the museum about next year/, where
it could wait until she wanted ideas for another supper. From the kitchen
window someone tapped on the glass and held up a cup. She waved. She had run
out of coffee half an hour ago.

The second tab of Sergio’s quote was in a message under a guest’s reply. She
opened it to see which hands he wanted to pay for. There were two times in
heavy type at the bottom. /Friday noon: final count./ Below it, /Wednesday
noon: release unbooked staff./

She looked at the calendar again. She’d copied Friday when the quote came in.
The two deadlines sat eight lines apart on her screen now. If she waited until
Friday to decide, she could tell him how many plates he needed. The people
who’d carry the plates would have jobs somewhere else.

She put Wednesday noon on the calendar. Then she drew a line through /room/ on
the loose envelope and wrote /Weller booked/ next to it. The envelope had
become a record of the week she’d thought she was going to have. At the top of
a clean page she wrote the result Ruth needed: /Winter routes paid for —
$14,000 net/. Below it she copied the offer from the back of an old ticket:
/Winter Supper. Three courses. Music. Doors at six./ She added /300 × $75/
because it mattered what people had paid. For the first time since the gym,
she took out the dinner drawing without looking for a way to fit another table
on it.

What did people get for seventy-five dollars? Some got the chance to sit down
together. Some came because they remembered standing in the pantry line, or
because they knew somebody who still did. There were three courses printed on
the ticket. Nobody had bought a ticket that said /whatever works for Maya/.

Last year, during the wait for dessert, Ruth had told the room how much it
cost to deliver a week’s groceries to one building. A table had asked for a
second microphone so a man at the far end could hear her better. Maya had sent
a runner out for one. She remembered being annoyed that Ruth had started
before she had the microphone ready. By the time it came, the table had gone
quiet to listen.

Maya turned over the floor plan. The two circles were still there. Each held a
hundred and fifty people. The first time around she’d put a dinner in each
one. What else could she put in them that people would pay to come to?
Something they could eat from one plate. Time to sit with the people they’d
come with. The music could stay. Ruth could talk in both rooms. They could say
exactly what the new ticket bought and return the money to anyone who wanted
the old dinner or couldn’t make the time.

Maya called Sergio before she could run it all the way through in her head.

“Can you price two receptions?” she said. “Seated if people want to sit. Light
food from a couple of stations. Enough that people can have a plate, and we
tell them to eat dinner somewhere else.”

“How many?”

“Up to a hundred and sixty in each. We’re going to offer refunds. Give me a
number that holds even if everyone comes.”

“Same end time?”

“Out at nine thirty. We’d have the guests out earlier.”

“You might get both out with the crew you have, plus a few. I’d want a clean
half hour between the groups.”

“Can you put the price and the crew on one page and send it by nine tomorrow?
I need Ruth to see it before you let anybody go.”

He said he could. Maya put /Sergio — reception quote, Wed 9/ where she could
find it and put a check at nine fifteen on her list for the morning. If she
didn’t hear, she’d call from Ruth’s office.

She was about to close the laptop when a new message from Jo arrived. Jo had
two possible walkthrough times on Thursday. One was at three, while the cable
company was replacing a box by the side door. That had just fallen through.
The other was six. A wedding crew took the room first thing Friday. Jo needed
somebody to stand in the room with her and sign the final plan before the
wedding crew put down its dance floor.

Maya opened the calendar for the second time that morning. It took one glance
to see whose name was already there.

* What Maya has just learned

Early on Tuesday, before anyone else is in, Maya lays out the notebook, the
laptop, the envelope and a paper bag from her coat pocket. She leaves the
unread mail alone and opens the calendar first. She deletes a reminder about a
hall she no longer has, sends one two-minute email, and parks an idea for next
year on a back page. Then she finds two deadlines eight lines apart, and the
earlier one is tomorrow. Because she has seen it in time, she can rethink what
she is offering.

* Your trusted system — Step 11: The first full review

/Fifteen to thirty minutes. This is the step that makes the rest work./

Do it somewhere quiet, in this order. Don't answer messages while you do it.

1. *Gather.* Empty pockets, bags, loose paper and phone notes into your capture
   place. Clarify each item as in steps 3–7.
2. *Calendar, back two weeks.* Does anything need a follow-up?
3. *Calendar, ahead four weeks.* What needs preparing? Check each deadline
   against its source document. Look for an earlier deadline hiding behind the
   one you wrote down.
4. *Next Actions.* Cross off what is done. Remove what no longer matters.
5. *Waiting For.* Does every line have a name and a check time? Is any overdue?
6. *Projects.* For each one: is the outcome still what I want, and what is its
   live step?
7. *Someday / Maybe.* Promote one item or delete one.
8. *Collisions.* Is there any day on which you have promised to be in two
   places?

Finish by writing one thing you now see that you didn't see before.

* The same step in Org Reason

Press ~C-c r w~. It opens this week's review file, =gtd/reviews/<year>-W<week>.org=,
with a checklist in the same order: Get clear, Get current, Get creative.
~C-c C-c~ on a line ticks its box. The file keeps your answers; reopening it
later in the week continues where you stopped.

Work with two views beside it:

- ~C-c r d~, the dashboard, for the inbox count, projects without a live step,
  and waiting entries without a person. Aim for zero, "None found", "None
  found".
- ~C-c r a~, the agenda, for the calendar, NEXT, WAIT, projects and SOMEDAY, in
  that order. In the agenda, ~b~ and ~f~ move back and forward a week.

Org Reason cannot see your paper, your email or your outside calendar. The
checklist's first lines remind you to look at them yourself.

Under =One decision for next week=, write the thing you now see. Under =What to
defer or stop=, write at least one line. Skip the optional learning section
this time.

- [ ] Inbox at zero, no stalled projects, a name on every WAIT.
- [ ] The review file has one decision and one thing to stop.

* Carry forward

Lists that are true as of today, a decision, and a rough idea of how long a
review takes you. You will need that number in step 17.
