# Volume 3 design: *Two Minutes at Full Speed*

Snowflake steps 1 to 5 in one file. Spoilers throughout.

The owner said "finish the novellas" on 12 November 2026 without choosing at
the gates, so the choices below were made by the writing agent. Each is
recorded so it can be reversed.

## Step 1. One sentence

Three candidates.

1. **Interpreter.** A translator who has collected every method for a sharper
   mind and practised none has ten weeks to follow two minutes of spoken
   German she can already read.
2. **Chess.** A club player who has bought every training course has one
   season to stop losing won positions.
3. **Surgeon.** A trainee who reads about expert performance has three months
   before an assessment of a procedure she has only watched.

**Chosen: 1.** It makes the score and the ability come apart cleanly: she
already knows the words on paper, so flashcards and a working-memory game
both reward her while the skill she lacks does not move. It also gives the
unscorable practice a natural place, because she cannot imagine what
following fast speech is like.

## Step 2. One paragraph

Maren Solberg, a translator with a box of unused brain-training methods,
agrees to interpret at a three-day audit ten weeks away and asks an expert
on practice which method has the best evidence. He makes her name one scene
and try it, and at a German conversation table she follows one word in five,
after her firm has already told the client she will do the job. While he is
away she fills her hour with a memory game and flashcards whose scores climb,
tells her manager not to book a professional, and three weeks later follows
no more than before, with the professional no longer available. He returns;
she learns to tell score from ability, seals a guess, changes the exercise,
and for her second complaint he shows her a practice with no evidence that
he has kept secret for nineteen years, which costs him his colleague's trust
and her manager's. She sorts everything she owns by the proof it owes, runs
the check and misses her own guess by one, does the job in the form she can
actually do, and is left knowing what she can do and not yet what it is for.

Disasters and the tension that causes each:

| Disaster | Cause |
|---|---|
| 1. One word in five, with the client already told | P1: reading in place of trying |
| 2. Scores up, the scene unchanged, the fallback gone | P4, and her own first fix |
| 3. The secret practice is found out | P5: the cost of working where there is no proof |

## Tension ledger

| # | One need, and who holds it | The other need, and who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Know enough before committing time: **Maren** | Begin, since only trying tells you about yourself: **Jutta** |
| P2 | Be guided and corrected by someone who knows: **Maren** | Start with what is actually available: **Pri** |
| P3 | Define the target before working toward it: **Ines** | Admit one cannot yet understand the ability one lacks: **Keane** |
| P4 | See progress, which keeps one going: **Wim** | Improve at the thing itself: **Jutta** |
| P5 | Demand proof and keep one's standing: **Ines** | Try what has no proof yet: **Keane** |
| P6 | Follow up every promising method: **Maren** | Give one method enough hours to tell: **Pri** |

Why each is right, in their own voice:

- **Jutta:** "I will not slow down. Nobody in that room will slow down. Better
  you find out from me, with cake."
- **Pri:** "You have ten weeks, an hour a day and a telephone. That is the
  whole budget. Spend it."
- **Ines:** "If you can't say what would count as better, you can't train it
  and I can't measure it. A feeling of progress is the cheapest thing there
  is."
- **Keane:** "You are asking a person to describe a room she has never been
  in. She can still tell you what she would be doing there."
- **Wim:** "Last March I couldn't button a shirt. A number that goes up is
  the reason I open the laptop. Don't take it off me until you've something
  better."
- **Keane, again:** "Everything now in the textbook was once in somebody's
  bottom drawer. Most of what's in bottom drawers is rubbish. Both are true."

## Steps 4 and 5, in brief

- **Part One: The Box** (chapters 1–4). She counts what she has collected,
  names one scene, lists twenty more, and tries the scene.
- **Part Two: The Score** (5–9). Keane leaves. She builds an exercise, a
  source of correction, an assistant as coach and a log, and then spends her
  hour on the two things that score.
- **Part Three: The Dark** (10–13). She separates score from ability, seals
  a guess, changes the exercise, and is shown the practice with no proof.
- **Part Four: The Check** (14–16). She sorts everything by the proof it
  owes, runs the check, and decides.

## What the story must not claim

- That the memory game or the flashcards harmed her. They took her hour.
- That image cycling helped her. Her log changes after she starts it and she
  cannot tell why. It stays "untested" to the last page.
- Anything specific about Bengston's work beyond this: he published
  experiments on healing in mice, and they do not concern thinking.
