# The story's reasoning: *The Fourth Visit*

Everything the characters reason out, in its final and its wrong forms. The
chapters quote from this file. It is written by hand and has not been entered
as a commons or run through the validator; see STATUS.md.

## Goal and conditions (chapters 2–3)

**Aim:** A fault a tenant reports is fixed soon and stays fixed.
**How we would know:** days from report to fixed, and whether the tenant has
to ring again.

Conditions, each necessary:

- C1. We know what the repair needs before we go.
- C2. The person and the parts that can finish it arrive together.
- C3. The tenant can let us in at a time they agreed.
- C4. The money lasts the year.

## Troubles (chapters 1 and 4)

First list, as Corinne wrote it, with blame: "technicians cut corners",
"tenants are never home", "stores never has anything".

Restated as facts against a condition:

- U1. Tenants wait 34 days for a repair. (the aim)
- U2. Open work orders have risen from 1,240 to 1,912 in a year. (the aim)
- U3. 31% of visits are coded "no access". (C3)
- U4. Technicians arrive without the part the job needs. (C2)
- U5. The wrong trade is sent. (C1)
- U6. Tenants ring again about the same fault; 35% of calls are repeats. (the aim)
- U7. Nobody knows how many visits a repair takes. (C1)

Dropped as irritations: the uniform order, the colour of the tenant app, the
depot car park.

## Causes (chapters 5–9)

Arrows as the characters reach them. "+" joins causes that are only enough
together.

- K1. The call centre books every report into the next free slot, at once.
- K2. The agent has three minutes and a script; nobody who could fix the fault
  hears it described.
- K3. So the job is booked before anyone knows what it needs. **(shared cause)**
- K3 → U5 (wrong trade sent).
- K3 + K4 (the van carries 85 lines, not 140) → U4 (no part).
- K3 + K5 (six jobs a day leaves no time to fetch a part or wait) → the visit
  ends without a repair.
- A visit that ends without a repair + K6 (a visit must be closed with a code,
  and "no access" is the code that asks no questions) → U3 in part.
- K1 + K7 (the slot is chosen without asking when the tenant is home) → U3 in
  part.
- Failed visits → 2.4 visits per repair (U7 made visible) → 260 repairs a week
  against 290 reports → U2 → U1.
- U1 → U6 → more calls → shorter calls (back to K2). A loop.

### The two wrong diagrams

1. **Chapter 4.** "Tenants are not home → 31% no access → backlog." A trouble
   used as a cause. Never tested.
2. **Chapter 7.** "Stock cut → no part → repeat visits → backlog." One arrow
   true, the conclusion false: the cause is not enough on its own (K4 needs
   K3), it began after the effect (March before June), and it accounts for 22%
   of repeat visits.

### The checks (chapter 8)

- If tenants were out, the tenants' own door log would show empty apartments.
  It shows someone home for 29 of 41.
- If "no access" meant no access, the van would wait. In 22 of those 29 it
  stayed under five minutes.
- If jobs go out unknown, then closed orders would show more than one visit
  each. 200 orders: 480 visits.

## The conflict (chapters 10–12)

- **Aim:** tenants' homes are repaired soon and stay repaired.
- **Need B (Lu):** every tenant who reports a fault knows quickly that it is
  being dealt with.
- **Need C (Mags):** every visit finishes the repair.
- **Action for B:** book a visit in the next free slot, at once.
- **Action for C:** hold the booking until the job is understood and its parts
  are in hand.

Becauses:

- B needs its action *because a tenant only believes they have been heard when
  a van is at the door.* (Corinne wrote this into the 2019 handbook.)
- C needs its action *because nobody can tell what a repair needs until a
  technician is standing in the room.*
- The two actions clash *because understanding a job takes days, so holding it
  means silence.*

**The compromise (wrong):** seven days and five-minute calls. Each side gets
less; the cause is untouched. Result: 78% and 2.3.

**The change:** the first conversation is with someone who can fix the fault.
The tenant shows it through their phone's camera or answers a technician's
questions, hears that day what is wrong, and is given one date. The visit is
booked once, with its parts. Understanding takes six minutes with the right
person; being heard means a name, a diagnosis and a date that is kept.

## Forward from the change (chapters 13–14)

- First call by a technician → the job is known → the right trade and parts go
  → the visit finishes the repair → visits per repair fall toward 1.4 → 300 or
  more repairs a week → the backlog falls → fewer repeat calls.

Harm, added by Lu in her own hand:

- Agents stop booking → they become message-takers → the best leave → calls
  are not answered in 20 seconds → tenants are back to 2018.
- **Altered change:** agents take the first call themselves for the twelve
  commonest faults, from question cards written with Mags; they own the
  promise of a call back within one working day; Mags's desk takes the rest.

Harm, added by Samira:

- Tenants without a camera phone are left out. **Altered:** a floor captain or
  the building's caretaker goes up with a phone.

Harm, added by Bram:

- Three technicians off the tools is 78 visits a week lost. Answered by the
  arithmetic, thinly, and accepted as a risk.

## Actions and what each should cause (chapter 16)

   the hearing it will take part.
2. One page to Gloria, each action beside its expected effect → the stop order
   is lifted for six buildings.
3. Wendell releases $38,000 → kits on six buildings' vans by Tuesday.
4. Lu's agents and Mags write the cards → agents take first calls on day one.
5. Bram reroutes Mags's jobs → the first-call desk opens Tuesday 16 December.
