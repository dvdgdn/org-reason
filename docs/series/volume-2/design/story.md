# Volume 2 design: *The Fourth Visit*

Snowflake steps 1 to 5 in one file. Spoilers throughout.

The owner said "finish the novellas" on 12 November 2026 without choosing at
the gates, so the choices below were made by the writing agent. Each is
recorded so it can be reversed.

## Step 1. One sentence

Three candidates.

1. **Housing repairs.** A housing manager with a growing repair backlog and no
   authority over the people who do the repairs has six weeks to find out why
   every improvement has made tenants wait longer.
2. **School.** A deputy head with one term before inspection must learn why
   each new support programme leaves more children behind in reading.
3. **Hospital.** A ward sister has a month to find out why the discharge lounge
   built to free beds has filled the corridors.

**Chosen: 1.** It presses T2 hardest, because every team's own number is
improving while the result gets worse, and it gives T1 a persuasive false
cause that blames the people being served. A school risks arguing about
education instead of reasoning; a hospital is ground other Theory of
Constraints novels already cover.

## Step 2. One paragraph

Corinne Vance is handed the repair backlog at Harbor Row Housing, where every
team is hitting its targets and tenants wait thirty-four days for a repair.
She gives the board a convincing explanation, that tenants are not home for
appointments, and the notices sent on the strength of it reach an
eighty-one-year-old man whose radiator her own staff failed to fix four times;
he is found cold in his apartment and taken to hospital. She then sets out to
prove the real cause is a stock cut, is taken apart in front of the director,
and learns that both explanations were partly true and that the contractor
hired on her first one has cost $24,000 for a backlog that is still rising,
while the tenants take the landlord to a city hearing. She finds the conflict
that keeps the real cause in place and a way to dissolve it, but the trial
breaks the one number the city watches and the director stops it three days
before the hearing. With the people who must change now holding the reasoning,
she offers the hearing a sealed prediction instead of a defence, is partly
wrong, and finds that the limit has moved.

Disasters and the tension that causes each:

| Disaster | Cause |
|---|---|
| 1. The notices and Aldo's hospital admission | T1: acting on the accepted explanation |
| 2. Her proof of the stock cut collapses; contractor money wasted; city complaint | T1 again, and rule 4: the diagram drawn to prove a belief |
| 3. The director stops the trial | T6 and T4: the real answer breaks a number someone relies on |

## Tension ledger

| # | One need, and who holds it | The other need, and who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Act now on an explanation everyone accepts: **Gloria** | Be right about the cause before spending: **Wendell** |

| T3 | Keep the peace with a compromise: **Corinne** | Meet both needs in full: **Samira** |
| T4 | Protect a fragile idea: **Corinne** | Find what it will break: **Bram**, then **Lu** |
| T5 | Work it out alone and fast: **Corinne** | Those who must change own the reasoning: **Lu**, **Samira** |
| T6 | Commit so people can rely on it: **Gloria** | Treat it as a prediction: **Odile**, **Wendell** |

Why each is right, in their own voice:

- **Gloria:** "A board that hears 'we don't know yet' three months running
  stops funding you. I need something true enough to act on by Thursday."
- **Wendell:** "We have paid for three explanations in two years. Each one was
  obvious. I would like the fourth to be checked first."
- **Bram:** "Eighteen months ago they said my people sat in vans. Now I can
  show five point two jobs a day. Nobody says that any more."
- **Mags:** "I can do five visits or I can do two repairs. Ask the tenant which
  one she rang about."
- **Lu:** "In 2018 people rang for a month and got a machine. Now a person
  answers in twenty seconds and gives them a day. Don't tell me that is the
  problem."
- **Samira:** "You are asking which we want, an answer or a repair. We pay rent
  for both."

## Steps 4 and 5, in brief

Four parts.

- **Part One: Busy** (chapters 1–4). Corinne learns to list troubles, name the
  aim and its conditions, and then takes a trouble to the board as if it were
  a cause.
- **Part Two: Two Explanations** (5–9). Odile is away. Corinne learns to join
  troubles into causes, misuses the method to convict the stock cut, is
  challenged properly, goes and looks, and finds the shared cause as the costs
  of the first explanation arrive.
- **Part Three: The Conflict** (10–14). She finds why the cause persists,
  splits the difference, is made to say her assumptions, finds the change, and
  meets the person it hurts.
- **Part Four: The Test** (15–18). The people who must change put the
  obstacles in order; she offers a prediction instead of a defence; the limit
  moves.

Each character's own version of the story is one in which they act sensibly:
Bram raised output, Lu ended silence, Wendell freed $212,000, Gloria got the
board a decision, and Corinne told them all something they could agree on.
