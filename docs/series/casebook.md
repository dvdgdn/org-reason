# The casebook

Short stories, one technique each, for the practices that volume 3 does not
treat in depth. The casebook is how the series keeps the full range of
techniques without crowding one novella.

Read [principles.md](principles.md), [snowflake.md](snowflake.md) and
[volume-3.md](volume-3.md) first. A case assumes nothing from volume 3 in its
story or its general steps, like any other volume.

## The cases

| Technique | Cell in volume 3's table | What makes it worth a case |
|---|---|---|
| Mnemonics and the memory palace | Scored, works for the skill practised | Fast, checkable gains that stay inside the material memorised |
| SMART relational training | Scored, wider benefit disputed | Promising small studies; a clean lesson in reading evidence |
| Blindfold chess and the Woodpecker method | Scored, works for the skill practised | Repetition of solved problems against the wish for novelty |
| Juggling | Scored, works for the skill practised | A physical skill with a count; reports of changes in the brain that are easy to over-read |
| Image streaming | Self-reported, untested | Large claims, no score; how to test one's own case |
| Multiple mentality | Self-reported, untested | An old course with no field behind it; practising with no teacher at all |

Order: the owner's own practice decides. A case is written only for a
technique the owner has practised for at least four weeks with records.

## The shape of a case

Each case is one volume in miniature and delivers the same four things.

1. **A story** of 4,000 to 6,000 words in three or four chapters. One main
   character, one tension from volume 3's ledger pressed hard, one disaster
   caused by it.
2. **Steps** after each chapter, in the series page format.
3. **One adapter or one written routine.** An adapter if a tool can score the
   practice ([software.md](software.md), package S7). A routine if only the
   practitioner can report it (package S9).
4. **One row** in the Practice panel and one row in the evidence ledger.

Every case contains these four steps, whatever else it adds:

| Step | The reader |
|---|---|
| The scene | Writes the real-world scene this technique is supposed to serve |
| The routine | Fixes what a session is, how long, and what is recorded |
| The sealed guess | Writes what they expect the real-world check to show, with a probability and a date |
| The check | Runs it and records the result whatever it is |

## Building a case

Use the Snowflake method in short form: steps 0, 1, 2, 3, 8 and 10, with one
gate after step 2. The tests for felt tension apply in full.

Before step 0:

- the technique's row in the evidence ledger is complete and its sources read;
- the owner's records for the technique exist;
- the adapter or routine is released.

## What a case must not do

- Claim more than its row in the ledger supports.
- Use a rising practice score as the happy ending. The ending is the
  real-world check, and in at least two of the six cases the check should
  disappoint.
- Teach a technique the series cannot describe accurately from primary
  sources. If the sources cannot be obtained, the case waits.
